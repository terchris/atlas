# Plan International Norge — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Stiftelsen Plan International Norge**, org.nr `976793382`. Plan Norge is the Norwegian member of the global **Plan International** federation (20 national organisations, 75+ programme countries, Global Hub in Woking, UK). In Norway it sits mid-pack in the humanitarian cluster by income (NOK 611.6 m in 2024) — smaller than SOS-barnebyer (668m) and Leger Uten Grenser (618m) by a hair, larger than Regnskogfondet (328m) — but it owns the **largest private-donor base of any Norwegian humanitarian after Redd Barna**: ~115 000 fadder-medlemskap.

In our framework Plan Norge is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): stiftelse, Oslo-only, no domestic chapters, no member democracy, no volunteer pathway. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces doesn't apply, so Plan Norge doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It does still appear in Compare NGOs, Funding transparency, and the Global-context panel — and this profile is the data source behind those surfaces.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, plan-norge.no and plan-international.org. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

Plan International's mandate is deliberately narrow compared to the other large Norwegian humanitarians: **children's rights, with a sharp focus on girls and young women aged 10–24**. The taxonomy, stable across all 20 national organisations in the federation, is five programme areas:

| Programme | Norwegian | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Education** | Utdanning | Free quality schooling, climate-smart education, teacher training, safe learning environments. Plan Norge cites 5.3 million girls reached in 2024 |
| **Child protection** | Barns beskyttelse | Prevention of violence against children; child protection in humanitarian crises; anti-trafficking |
| **Sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR)** | Seksuell og reproduktiv helse | Access to information, services, contraception; maternal health for young mothers |
| **Ending child marriage and gender-based violence** | Mot barneekteskap og kjønnsbasert vold | Core Norad framework theme; includes FGM work (e.g. The Gambia FGM ban advocacy) |
| **Economic empowerment and youth leadership** | Økonomisk selvstendighet og ungdomsledelse | Youth livelihoods, vocational training, girl-led advocacy, leadership programmes |

Plan Norge is emphatic on distinguishing itself from other child-focused organisations: the strategy is **girls-first**, not child-generalist. In 2024 over half the 49 million people reached were girls; humanitarian work (87 crisis responses) reached 12.2 million people of whom 3.2 million were girls.

Source: plan-norge.no/om-plan, Årsrapport 2024.

### Flagship initiatives

- **#GirlsGetEqual** — Plan International's multi-year global campaign, launched on International Day of the Girl (11 October). The Norwegian chapter runs its version around the same annual moment, with #JenterTarOver as its local framing. Over five years globally the campaign has produced ~6 000 Girls' Takeovers and over 1 300 leader commitments. The Norad framework agreement is branded under the same name ("Girls Get Equal: Preventing Child, Early and Forced Marriage").
- **Girls Takeover (Jenter tar over)** — youth-led leadership days where girls step into roles as ministers, CEOs, newsroom editors, or ambassadors for a day. Norwegian takeovers have included the statsminister's chair and major media desks. Plan Norge co-runs these with corporate and public partners annually in October.
- **Fadder-programmet (Child Sponsorship)** — Plan's original and still-largest engagement programme. A Norwegian fadder does **not** give cash directly to a specific child; contributions go to the community the sponsored child lives in, and the sponsor receives annual updates, photos, and can exchange digital messages via "Min side". This is by design — Plan's stated rationale is that direct cash to named children would create "unjust distinctions" inside a community. ~115 000 Norwegian fadder-medlemskap support ~1.1 million sponsored children globally through Plan Norge's share of the federation's fadder-pool.
- **Because I am a Girl** — Plan International's longer-running global advocacy platform on girls' rights; Girls Get Equal is the current campaign iteration.
- **Postkodelotteriet partnership** — Plan Norge is one of 23 beneficiary organisations receiving minimum 50% of Postkodelotteriet's proceeds (Norsk Postkodelotteri AS). Material but not flagship-scale.

## Where they work

The Plan International federation is active in **75+ countries** (82 by internal count including liaison offices). Plan Norge's direct programme footprint — the countries where its Norad and UD-funded programmes land — is much narrower and is driven by the **Girls Get Equal framework agreements**.

| Framework agreement | Amount | Duration | Countries |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Girls Get Equal 2020–2024** ("Preventing Child, Early and Forced Marriage") | NOK 246.4 m | 2020–2024 | Bangladesh, Malawi, Nepal, Niger, Tanzania, Uganda |
| **Girls Get Equal 2025–2029** | NOK 255 m | 2025–2029 | Bangladesh, Ethiopia, Malawi, Mozambique, Nepal, Niger, Tanzania |
| **Quality Education and Protection from Violence (UD)** | undisclosed | 2021–2023 | Burkina Faso, Mali, Niger (Sahel humanitarian) |
| **A Working Future** (Uganda, youth livelihoods) | undisclosed | 2018–2022 | Uganda |
| **Together for Inclusion** (Atlas Alliance consortium) | undisclosed share | 2019–2022 | Niger + four other countries |

Source: plan-norge.no/om-plan/stotte-fra-norad-og-ud.

The 2020–2024 agreement reported reaching 317 000 children and youth across 741 schools and 544 communities. The 2025–2029 successor broadens the pillars to include **climate-smart education, SRHR, economic strengthening, and civil society** and swaps Uganda out for Ethiopia and Mozambique.

For countries where Plan Norge doesn't run direct programmes but the federation does (e.g. Colombia, Peru, Philippines), Norwegian fadder-contributions flow through the federation's shared programme structure. ## Money in — funding composition

This is the structural detail that matters for Jonas (transparency-focused donor) and Ola (data-curious observer): Plan Norge is **overwhelmingly private-donor funded**, the inverse of NRC's institutional-dominated mix.

### Income by source (FY2024)

| Source | Share | Type |
|---|---:|---|
| **Fadder + other private donors** | **~76%** | Private Norwegian individuals |
| **Norad + UD + EU + UN + other institutional** | **~20%** | State / multilateral |
| **Corporate, foundations, organisations, one-off private** | **~4%** | Private non-individual |

Source: plan-norge.no/om-plan/slik-fordeles-inntektene; SNL cross-reference.

### What this tells us

- **Plan Norge is a private-donor-led organisation first and a Norad-grant recipient second.** The opposite pattern from NRC (where US BHA alone is 17% and private Norwegian giving is a small slice).
- **The fadder-base is the dominant revenue engine.** 115 000 fadder × an average of ~350 NOK/month × 12 months = ~483 m/year in pure fadder-contributions (before attrition/partial-year); combined with other private giving this reaches the ~465m private slice of 611.6m total income. The arithmetic is tight but internally consistent.
- **Norad's ~60m/year framework (Girls Get Equal) is ~10% of income.** Adding UD humanitarian grants (Sahel) and EU/UN flows gets to ~20% institutional share. This is a **much lower** state-dependency than Kirkens Nødhjelp (~25%), Norsk Folkehjelp (~40%) or Regnskogfondet (~80%).
- **Postkodelotteriet and corporate partners** contribute a measurable but small slice (~4%).

### Funding flows outside Lottstift

Like NRC, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Folkehjelp and Redd Barna, Plan Norge's Norad income flows through a **direct framework agreement** with Norad's Department for Civil Society (formerly Section for Equality), not through `tilskudd.lottstift.no` ordninger. A naive Lottstift query for 976793382 will return little — the ~60m/year Norad money lives in Statsbudsjettet kap. 151–160 Norad rammeavtaler. Same pattern as the other operational humanitarians. See `ngo-landscape.md` §4 caveats.

## The fadder-model economics — why share-to-cause is 76.5%

This deserves its own section because it's the single most distinctive number in Plan Norge's profile and the one most likely to confuse a donor comparing "efficiency" across humanitarians.

### The numbers

| Org (Tier C) | Share-to-cause 2024 | Private-donor share | Model |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **NRC / Flyktninghjelpen** | 97.2% | low | Institutional, few large donors |
| **Norsk Folkehjelp** | 96.1% | moderate | Norad frames + fagbevegelsen + Norsk Tipping |
| **CARE Norge** | 95.4% | low | UD/Norad/SIDA/EU |
| **Redd Barna** | 92.8% | moderate | Fadder + Norad frame + legacies |
| **Leger Uten Grenser** | 89.0% | 100% | Pure private, policy-bound |
| **Kirkens Nødhjelp** | 88.9% | moderate | Norad frame + menigheter |
| **SOS-barnebyer** | 84.5% | dominant | ~100 000 sponsors |
| **Plan International Norge** | **76.5%** | **dominant (~76%)** | **~115 000 fadder + Norad frame** |
| **UNICEF-komiteen Norge** | 66.3% | 100% | Private only, no state |

Plan Norge's 76.5% is the second-lowest on this list. The pattern is clear: **share-to-cause varies inversely with the share of income coming from individual private donors**. The more your income depends on retaining a large base of small monthly givers, the higher your fundraising and relationship-maintenance costs.

### Why the fadder-model is structurally more expensive

Four reasons — all visible in Plan Norge's own cost line items (115.3m fundraising + 28.1m admin on 611.6m income = 23.5% non-programme):

1. **Acquisition cost.** Recruiting a new fadder — the classic ask to commit 300–400 NOK/month indefinitely — requires street fundraising, TV spots, digital acquisition funnels, and phone follow-up. Industry rule of thumb: first-year fadder contributions roughly cover acquisition cost; real programme margin starts in year 2+. With ~10–15% annual attrition, a meaningful slice of gross fundraising income is consumed re-recruiting.
2. **Per-sponsor servicing.** Each fadder expects an annual update, photos of "their" child, ability to send digital messages, tax-receipt handling, payment-method maintenance, and a direct service line. At 115 000 sponsors this is a non-trivial back-office operation. Plan's own FAQ notes administration is ~4% of collected amounts — i.e. the admin line is explicitly priced into the fadder relationship.
3. **Per-child tracking in-country.** The sponsored-child model requires Plan's country offices to maintain individual child records, annual updates, and case-level integrity across 1.1 million sponsored children globally. This is real per-child overhead that institutional-grant-funded humanitarians (NRC, Folkehjelp on Norad) simply don't carry.
4. **Marketing and brand retention.** Retaining a 10–20-year fadder relationship against churn pressure requires ongoing brand marketing that an institutional-grant-dominated organisation (NRC: 1.5% fundraising cost) doesn't need to run.

UNICEF-komiteen at 66.3% and SOS-barnebyer at 84.5% both sit below the 90% bar for the same structural reason — their income model is built on retaining and servicing large bases of individual sponsors.

### What this means for Jonas's "which org is most efficient" question

A naive read — "Plan spends only 77 øre of every krone on the cause, NRC spends 97 øre" — is wrong in isolation. The right framing is:

- **If the question is "how much of the average krone reaches a beneficiary"**: NRC and Folkehjelp win because institutional donors pre-select the cause and fundraising is cheap.
- **If the question is "how much money exists for girls' rights in Norway that wouldn't exist without this organisation"**: Plan Norge's 115 000 fadder-base represents ~465m/year in private Norwegian donations that wouldn't be channelled at all without the sponsorship infrastructure. A 76.5% formålsprosent on 465m private money is ~355m reaching programmes — a number NRC would need very substantial institutional wins to match on private Norwegian donations alone.
- **The 76.5% is a cost of donor democratisation, not of inefficiency.** Plan and SOS-barnebyer are how small givers get leverage. That infrastructure has a price.

For the app's transparency UI, this means **raw share-to-cause should not be the headline metric for comparing Tier C orgs**. Pair it with "private-share of income" and "implied private NOK reaching cause" for honest comparison.

## Plan vs Redd Barna — brief cross-reference

Both are child-focused. Both lean fadder-heavy. Different missions.

| Dimension | Plan International Norge | Redd Barna (Save the Children Norway) |
|---|---|---|
| Income 2024 | 611.6 m | 1 690 m (2.8× larger) |
| Fadder-base | ~115 000 | ~100 000 (roughly comparable in scale) |
| Focus | **Girls specifically** (10–24) | Children broadly, all genders |
| Domestic programmes | None (pure international) | Yes — Norwegian child-poverty, school programmes, regional offices, aktivitetsgrupper |
| Structure | Stiftelse, Oslo-only, no chapters | Stiftelse with 3 regions + partial lokallag / aktivitetsgrupper |
| Share-to-cause | 76.5% | 92.8% |
| Tier | C | Between B and C (partial chapters) |
| Norad framework | Girls Get Equal, ~60m/year | Larger, multi-thematic |
| Flagship | Girls Takeover, Girls Get Equal | Barnas Dag, Joy Kondoy fadderprogram, fattigdomsrapporter |

**Why the share-to-cause gap (76.5% vs 92.8%)?** Redd Barna's income mix is more institutional — bigger Norad framework, large legacies from TV-aksjonen history, more corporate. Plan's is more purely individual-sponsor-driven, so fundraising cost as % of income is higher. Plan's fadder-base per NOK raised is also smaller than Redd Barna's (both have ~100–115k sponsors, but Redd Barna's 1.69bn total means institutional dollars dilute the per-sponsor fundraising cost).

**Substitutability from a donor's perspective.** A donor choosing between the two is effectively choosing:
- **Plan** for a narrower, girls-and-young-women focus, international-only, more activist framing (Girls Takeover)
- **Redd Barna** for a broader children-rights scope that includes domestic Norwegian advocacy and a larger institutional/humanitarian operational footprint

The Norwegian-gift mechanism ("bli fadder") is almost identical — 300–400 NOK/month, annual child updates, digital messages, 22% skattefradrag on contributions 500–25 000 NOK/year. Both organisations have faced similar public-critique episodes over abrupt country closures (Plan's 2022 Sri Lanka exit made headlines via Bistandsaktuelt's SKUP-nominated investigation).

## How to engage

Plan Norge is **donor-only** for the Norwegian public. No member, no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no chapter network. The fadder relationship is transactional (monthly payment + annual updates), not volunteering.

Verified pathways (plan-norge.no/bidra):

| Pathway | URL / route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Bli fadder** | `/bidra/bli-fadder` | Primary ask; 300/350/400 NOK/month suggested. Flagship product |
| Fastgiver (recurring, not fadder) | `/bidra/fastgiver` | Monthly gift, no named child |
| One-off donation | `/bidra/donasjon` | Vipps 10165 |
| Nødhjelp (emergency) | `/bidra/livreddende-nodhjelp` | Gaza Vipps 1133; general 10165 |
| Crowdfunding / personal fundraising | `/bidra/innsamlingsaksjon` | Spleis + Vipps, birthday/event campaigns |
| Gaveshop (gaver med mening) | `/bidra/gaver-med-mening` | Symbolic gifts |
| Minnegave / arv | `/bidra/arv-og-testamentariske-gaver` | Memorial + bequest |
| Stor gave / filantropi | `/bidra/storre-gave` | Bespoke major-gift structure |
| Næringsliv / corporate | `/bidra/bedrifter` | Corporate partnerships |
| Skole / barnehage | `/skoler` | Educational-institution partnerships (not volunteering) |
| Tax deduction | 500–25 000 NOK/year qualifies for skattefradrag | Norwegian rules |
| **Girls Takeover / takeover days** | Annual October event | Youth-led, not public-volunteer slot |

Why no volunteer pathway? Three structural reasons, same as NRC:

1. **Operational model.** Plan is a professional children's-rights operator implemented through the global federation's 50+ country offices. Programme delivery is paid in-country staff, not Norwegian volunteers.
2. **Stiftelse form.** Foundations don't have members; no governance slot for volunteers.
3. **Where the volunteer ask goes.** Norwegians wanting girls'-rights volunteer work are systematically pointed toward FOKUS, CARE Norge advocacy groups, or global solidarity networks — not Plan's programme delivery.

For the app, this means Plan Norge's Compare-NGOs card should explicitly say **"donasjonsbasert organisasjon — fadder er primærpathway, ingen frivilligplassering"** rather than show an empty volunteer slot.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Share-to-cause is a real 76.5%, not an artefact.** Unlike Barnekreftforeningen's transient 49% (TV-aksjonen spending tail), Plan's 76.5% is structural — it will not move materially without a change in income mix.
- **Fiscal-year mismatch.** Plan Norge reports on a 1 July – 30 June year. Innsamlingskontrollen figures for "2024" reflect FY July 2023 – June 2024. Comparisons against NRC (calendar year) carry a ~6-month offset. For the app's year-on-year charts, label the fiscal convention clearly.
- **Generalsekretær transition in progress.** Partapuoli leaving for Sanitetskvinnene; Fylkesnes starts 2026-06-01. The app should show "Ny fra juni 2026: Gunvor Knag Fylkesnes" in the period between announcement and start.
- **Programme-by-programme expenditure split not extracted.** Live annual report PDF (2025 edition) parsed as binary, not text. Programme and country-level expenditure exists in the PDF but requires headless-browser or PDF-to-text extraction. Worth running offline once.
- **Priority-countries page 404'd.** `plan-norge.no/hva-vi-gjor/hvor-vi-jobber` not live on 2026-04-18. Country list constructed from Norad framework agreement text instead. Should be re-verified before production.
- **Ansatte og styret page 404'd.** Full board roster not extracted. Styreleder (Holmås) and generalsekretær (Partapuoli → Fylkesnes) confirmed via separate sources; board members below need a follow-up fetch when page returns.
- **Founding-year ambiguity.** Brreg says 1996-08-05 (current legal entity registered as stiftelse). Plan International globally founded 1937. App should display "Founded 1996 (Plan International globally 1937)" with footnote.
- **Fadder arithmetic.** 115 000 × 350 NOK × 12 = 483 m implied annual fadder contributions. FY2024 total income is 611.6m with ~76% from private individuals = ~465m. The numbers reconcile but imply average fadder contribution is closer to 335 NOK/month, or that effective paying-fadder count (after attrition, partial-year new joiners, payment failures) is ~110 000 effective. Order-of-magnitude fine; don't overclaim precision.
- **2022 Sri Lanka exit.** Plan Norge drew significant Bistandsaktuelt criticism in 2022 for abruptly withdrawing from Sri Lanka with 20 000 active fadderbarn. Worth noting as governance-history context though not currently a live reputational issue.
- **Fadder vs federation economics.** Plan Norge's fadder-contributions flow partly to the global federation and partly to Plan Norge's own Norad/UD-matched programmes. Exact inter-company flows (Plan Norge → Plan International Global Hub) not extracted. The 76.5% share-to-cause is before considering inter-federation transfers — which are programme-costs from Plan Norge's accounting perspective.
