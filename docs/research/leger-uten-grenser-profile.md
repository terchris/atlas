# Leger Uten Grenser Norge / MSF — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Leger Uten Grenser Norge / Médecins Sans Frontières Norway**, org.nr `977097495`. LUG is a mid-sized Norwegian humanitarian (NOK 618m income, 2024) but a structural outlier in the sector: it is the most prominent Norwegian NGO that **refuses Norwegian state funding by policy** — a stance held unbroken since 2016 — and its entire engagement pathway is built around either cash giving or paid field deployment, with no volunteer, no member-donor, and no chapter surface.

In our framework LUG is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a professional humanitarian operator with no domestic chapter network, no kommune-level presence, and no general-public volunteer pathway. Unlike NRC (stiftelse), LUG is formally an **association (FLI)** — but the association is restricted to people who have served as field workers or staff, so in practice it looks donor-first to the Norwegian public. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces (Red Cross, Sanitetskvinnene, Nasjonalforeningen, Folkehjelp, etc.) doesn't apply, so LUG doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It does appear in three places in the app — Compare NGOs, Funding transparency (where its 0% state-funding share is the anchor story), and the Global-context panel during active crises — and this profile is the data source behind those surfaces.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, legerutengrenser.no, sml.snl.no, Wikipedia, msf.org, and panoramanyheter.no. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

MSF delivers **emergency medical humanitarian action** in crisis settings. The programme taxonomy is stable across the global movement and maps to six recurring areas of intervention (Norwegian labels taken from legerutengrenser.no):

| Programme | Norwegian | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Conflict and displacement medicine** | Krigs- og konfliktmedisin | Trauma surgery, emergency rooms, mass-casualty response in active conflict zones (Gaza, Sudan, Ukraine, DRC, Yemen) |
| **Epidemic response** | Epidemi- og pandemirespons | Outbreak response: Ebola, cholera, measles, Mpox, COVID-19. Vaccination campaigns at scale |
| **Maternal and child health** | Mødre- og barnehelse | Safe births, neonatal care, paediatrics, vaccination — core programming in chronic-crisis contexts |
| **HIV / TB / malaria** | Smittsomme sykdommer | Long-horizon infectious-disease care, drug-resistant TB research, ARV rollout |
| **Malnutrition** | Underernæring | Therapeutic feeding programmes (RUTF), nutrition surveillance |
| **Migration health** | Migrasjonshelse | Medical services along migration routes, search-and-rescue (Mediterranean), mental-health support |

Source: legerutengrenser.no/om-oss/dette-gjor-leger-uten-grenser + MSF IAR 2024.

### Témoignage — the witnessing mandate

MSF is one of the few humanitarian operators with an **explicit advocacy mandate built into its charter**: the French word *témoignage* ("bearing witness") is codified alongside medical care. MSF teams publicly document what they see — displacement, abuses of humanitarian law, denial of access — and use that testimony to push for political response. Examples from recent years: reporting on Gaza healthcare access (active through 2025–2026, with January 2026 public challenge to new Israeli access restrictions), calls to action on Sudan, challenges to EU migration policy.

This is structurally distinctive. NRC does advocacy through institutional channels (UN briefings, op-eds). MSF uses field testimony as a public-facing instrument, and the Norwegian generalsekretær frequently speaks in Norwegian media on specific crises. It also feeds directly into **why MSF declines state funding** — see the dedicated section below.

## Where they work

MSF Norway is a **partner section** of **Operational Centre Brussels (OCB)** — one of MSF's five Operational Centres (Amsterdam, Barcelona-Athens, Brussels, Geneva, Paris). OCB's partner sections are Belgium (OC lead), Brazil, Denmark, Hong Kong, Italy, Luxembourg, **Norway**, South Africa, Sweden, plus branch offices in Finland, Lebanon, and Taiwan. This matters structurally:

- **Norway is not operational on its own.** Field operations are run by the Operational Centres. MSF Norway contributes money, recruits Norwegian field workers for international deployment, and runs Norwegian fundraising + advocacy.
- **Programming decisions route via OCB**, not via Oslo. The Norwegian section's role is funding + recruitment + advocacy, not country-by-country operational control.
- **The 70+ / 75+ country figure is the global MSF number**, not a Norway-specific list. Annually recurring country presence includes: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cameroon, CAR, Chad, Colombia, DRC, Ethiopia, Gaza/OPT, Haiti, Honduras, Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Mali, Mexico, Mozambique, Myanmar, Niger, Nigeria, Pakistan, Palestine, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Venezuela, Yemen, and others.

Source: MSF movement pages + MSF IAR 2024.

Implication for our app: LUG's entry in the Global-context panel should key off **MSF global** country presence, not a Norway-specific list. Where an active crisis is in a country where MSF operates, surface MSF alongside NRC, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Folkehjelp, and IFRC appeals.

## Why no state funding — the 2016 policy

This is LUG's defining governance decision and deserves its own section. It is the single data point that most distinguishes LUG from every other large Norwegian humanitarian.

### The trigger

In March 2016 the **EU signed an agreement with Turkey** (the "EU-Turkey deal") under which Turkey would stop migrants from crossing to the EU in exchange for €6 bn in support. MSF publicly judged the deal as "*a cynical and inhuman agreement that violated humanitarian principles, including people's real right to seek asylum.*" (Christian Sørensen, MSF Norway fundraising director, quoted in Panoramanyheter.)

MSF's global response was to **stop accepting funding from EU institutions and EU member states**. Because Norway aligns with EU migration policy (Dublin / Schengen / EØS), MSF Norway extended the principle and stopped seeking funds from the Norwegian state as well.

### Pre-2016 baseline (what they gave up)

In the year before the decision, MSF received globally (EU-related):

- €19 m from EU institutions
- €37 m from EU member states
- €6.8 m from Norwegian authorities (roughly NOK 60 m at 2015 exchange rate)
- **Combined ~€62.8 m / NOK 565 m**

That was the scale of the institutional grant stream MSF walked away from. MSF has replaced it with private donor growth, not with a shrunk programme.

### The broader principle

Before 2016, MSF had already declined state funding from specific donors where it compromised operational neutrality — notably refusing NATO-country grants for Afghanistan work. The 2016 decision generalised the principle: state funding, especially from states that are parties to ongoing conflicts or to contested migration regimes, risks being read by local parties (governments, armed groups, communities) as tied to donor-state interests. In negotiating access with both government forces and opposition groups in conflict zones, perceived independence is operational survival.

### Current funding composition

From MSF Norway's own reporting and Panoramanyheter's verification: **97–98% private donor share**. For 2022 the split was approximately NOK 489 m private out of NOK 500 m total. For 2024 the NOK 618 m total income is, per MSF's public communication, "*in all essentials from private contributors*" (i all hovedsak fra private bidragsytere).

Verified pathways into that private share: AvtaleGiro månedsgivere ("Feltpartner"), Vipps fixed payments, one-off Vipps (2177), SMS giving, arv/testamentariske gaver, corporate partnerships, TV-aksjonen 2022 (LUG + DNDi, NOK 266 m), minnegaver.

### Why this matters for the app

For transparency-focused personas (Jonas, Ola), **LUG is the Norwegian sector's reference case for privately-funded operational humanitarian work**. In a Compare-NGOs surface that shows "share from Norwegian state" or "institutional vs private donor split", LUG is the anchor at one end of the axis and the chapter federations (Red Cross ~16%, Folkehjelp, Blå Kors) and operational stiftelser (NRC 20%+ Norwegian state plus 50%+ non-Norwegian state) sit at the other. The sector is bimodal and LUG is the cleanest example of the privately-funded pole.

### Comparable Norwegian orgs with similar policies

Only one other major Norwegian NGO follows a comparable private-only discipline: **UNICEF-komiteen Norge** (NOK 250m income, 100% private, no Norwegian state money — see ngo-landscape.md §4.1). UNICEF's position is structural (komiteer fund UNICEF programmes; they're not operational), whereas LUG's is a deliberate post-2016 policy. Other major orgs (NRC, Folkehjelp, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Redd Barna, Plan, Regnskogfondet, CARE) all take substantial state grants through Lottstift and/or Norad/UD framework agreements.

## Money in — funding composition

The single-line view is simple: **~97–98% private donors, ~0% Norwegian state**. The finer breakdown across 2022–2024:

| Year | Total income (NOK m) | Direct purpose (NOK m) | Fundraising (NOK m) | Admin (NOK m) | Notes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2024 | 618.1 | 550.4 | 104.0 | 20.3 | Private-donor dominated |
| 2023 | 568.4 | 506.3 | 89.9 | 18.5 | Private-donor dominated |
| 2022 | 769.0 | 433.8 | 108.4 | 19.9 | TV-aksjonen 2022 added NOK 266m (LUG + DNDi partnership); spending tail continues into 2023/2024 |

Source: Innsamlingskontrollen (live, 2026-04-18).

The 2022 spike is the **TV-aksjonen 2022** partnership between MSF Norway and DNDi ("Tusen takk!" — record NOK 266 m raised). Under Norwegian fundraising accounting this is booked as revenue in the year received, with programme spend following in subsequent years — which compresses the 2022 share-to-cause ratio and inflates the 2023–2024 baselines.

### Top funding flows (qualitative, from 2022–2024 MSF reporting)

- **Fast givere / "Feltpartnere"** (AvtaleGiro + Vipps faste trekk) — the largest and most stable income stream. MSF Norway explicitly builds the donor pyramid around this cohort.
- **Large one-offs + enkeltbeløp** via Vipps 2177 and Gi nå-flow.
- **Arv / testamentariske gaver** — significant annual contribution (specific share not published).
- **Corporate partnerships** — multi-year næringslivspartnere.
- **TV-aksjonen 2022** (one-off, partnership with DNDi).
- **Private trusts, legacies, stiftelser** — supplementary.
- **Minnegaver** — funeral-context giving.

### What's deliberately missing

No UD rammeavtale. No Norad framework agreement. No Lottstift momskompensasjon claim (MSF's status re momskomp needs re-verification; most Innsamlingskontrollen-member orgs are eligible but LUG's policy on this is not immediately clear from public pages). No TV-aksjonen outside the 2022 partnership year. No EU ECHO, no BHA. This is a structural absence, not an oversight.

For the funding-transparency UX: **any surface that filters on "institutional donor share" should show LUG at effectively 0%** — the cleanest signal in the Norwegian NGO sector.

## Money out — by programme and geography

LUG publishes:
- Total direct purpose costs 2024: **NOK 550.4 m** (89.0% of income on gross direct-purpose basis)
- Fundraising costs 2024: **NOK 104.0 m** (16.8% of income — higher than NRC's 1.5% because private-donor model is fundraising-intensive)
- Administration 2024: **NOK 20.3 m** (3.3%)

Programme + geographic split exists in the `arsregnskap-leger-uten-grenser-norge-{year}.pdf` on Innsamlingskontrollen but PDFs parsed as binary in this verification pass. For country-level expenditure, the better source is **MSF's International Activity Report 2024** (msf.org) which breaks down consultations, staff, and spend by country at the global-movement level — Norwegian share is a proportional contribution to OCB's budget.

Methodological note on fundraising ratio: the 16.8% fundraising cost share reflects the structural cost of the private-donor model. Competitor operators that take institutional grants (NRC 1.5%, Folkehjelp, Kirkens Nødhjelp) have much lower fundraising ratios because institutional money is grant-application cheap relative to the cost of acquiring and retaining 97%-private-donor income. The trade-off is baked into the 2016 decision.

## How to engage

LUG is **donor-first + recruitment-second** for the Norwegian public. There is no member-democracy pathway for donors, no lokallag, no volunteer programme, and no school-ambassador / student-ambassador model. The association (the formal member body under FLI status) is restricted to people who have served as MSF field workers or staff.

### Field-worker recruitment (unique to MSF)

This is the **only major Norwegian humanitarian where a medical professional can move from "donor" to "deployed aid worker" through a formal recruitment pipeline**. It is employment-based (paid contract), not volunteering.

Structure:

| Role cluster | Examples | Norwegian landing page |
|---|---|---|
| Lege | Allmennlege, kirurg, anestesilege, pediater | legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/lege |
| Sykepleier | Operasjons, akuttmottak, infeksjon, pediatri | legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/sykepleier |
| Jordmor | Fødselshjelp i krise | legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/ |
| HR og ledelse | Field coordinator, project coordinator, HR-leder | legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/hr-og-ledelse |
| Driftspersonell | Logistikk, vann/sanitet, supply | legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/driftspersonell |

Contract terms (from legerutengrenser.no/jobb-for-oss/sporsmal-og-svar/):

- **Minimum first deployment: 6–12 months.**
- **Base salary: minimum NOK 24 095 brutto/month** (MSF international pay scale, adjusted for role and experience).
- **Daglig kostgodtgjørelse** in local currency for food, drink, personal expenses in-country.
- **Five days leave after three months** on standard contracts; on <6-month contracts paid out rather than taken.
- **Application typically ~8 months before availability window.**
- Annually ~100–130 Norwegian field workers deployed.

Implication for the app: LUG needs a **"Bli feltarbeider"** pathway alongside "Gi penger" — this is a unique-to-MSF engagement surface in the Norwegian NGO landscape. The framework's three-pathway schema (Gi tid / Gi penger / Bli medlem) needs extending to a fourth: **Jobb i felt** (paid deployment). The Red Cross analogue is Beredskap/Hjelpekorps volunteering, but those are domestic and unpaid; MSF field recruitment is international and paid. The two models shouldn't be conflated.

### Why no volunteer pathway

Three structural reasons:

1. **Operational model.** MSF is a professional medical operator; field staff are paid employees (national or international), deployed through HR with rigorous qualification and security vetting. Untrained volunteers don't fit the model, and the medical qualification bar is high.
2. **Association structure.** The FLI form with restricted association membership (field workers only) means the formal "member" pool is small and experience-gated. General-public membership isn't offered.
3. **Oslo HQ has a support function**, not a field function. The ~209 Oslo employees are fundraising, communications, recruitment, HR, finance, advocacy. There's no Norwegian-facing programme to volunteer into.

Norwegian medical professionals who want to contribute time (rather than money) are directed to the field recruitment pipeline. Non-medical volunteers are directed elsewhere — Røde Kors, Folkehjelp, kommune integration partners.

## Governance

- **Form**: Forening/lag/innretning (FLI) under Brreg; the corporate-law status for non-stiftelse, non-AS civil associations. Regulated by general association law + the organisation's vedtekter.
- **Generalsekretær**: **Lindis Hurum**, since October 2021. 15+ years as MSF emergency coordinator before the role; master's in Disaster Management (University of Copenhagen). Active public voice on Gaza, Sudan, Ukraine.
- **Styreleder**: **Dina Hovland**, jordmor, since 2023. Elected from the association at annual general meeting. Prior MSF assignments in Ethiopia, Somalia, Sierra Leone, Uganda, Somaliland, South Sudan, Mozambique, Libya, Pakistan, Malawi, Zimbabwe. Started women's health project for undocumented migrants at Ullevål.
- **Board**: 9 members (6 women / 3 men, average age 47). Elected from the association membership (all have MSF field or staff experience).
- **Association (the member body)**: Norway is one of 26 MSF associations globally. Membership is restricted to people who have served as MSF field workers, staff, or equivalent. Members shape strategic direction at the annual general meeting.

### MSF Norway's place in the movement

- MSF International is coordinated by an **International Board** representing sections and associations.
- MSF operates through **5 Operational Centres (OCs)**: Amsterdam, Barcelona-Athens, Brussels, Geneva, Paris.
- **Norway is a partner section of OCB (Brussels)** alongside Belgium, Brazil, Denmark, Hong Kong, Italy, Luxembourg, South Africa, Sweden (+ Finland, Lebanon, Taiwan branch offices).
- Norwegian money and Norwegian recruited staff flow into OCB's programming decisions and budget.

### Governance documents

MSF's charter, Chantilly Principles, and La Mancha Agreement form the international governance framework. MSF Norway also publishes årsberetning and årsregnskap annually via Innsamlingskontrollen. Core humanitarian principles — neutrality, impartiality, independence, bearing witness — are codified across the movement.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Share-to-cause discrepancy.** `ngo-landscape.md` cites **89.0%** (Innsamlingskontrollen formula: direct-purpose / total income = 550.4 / 618.1 = 89.1%). MSF's own 2024 reporting says **79%** — a different denominator (likely including investment/financial flows or applying a stricter exclusion of international transfer costs). Both are defensible; label methodology in UI. The 82.6% 5-year innsamlingsprosent is yet another method (income ÷ ordinary cost of fundraising). Pick one, show the method.
- **"70+ vs 75+ countries" inconsistency.** MSF's own live material says 70+; IAR 2024 headline figure is 75+. The ngo-landscape.md baseline says 70+. Use 70+ for Norwegian-facing narrative (matches LUG's own phrasing) and 75+ for app precision where IAR 2024 is cited.
- **Field-worker count.** Sources vary: "100 assignments" vs "130 assignments" per year from Norway. These may reflect different counting (per deployment vs per person vs FTE). Use "100–130 per year" in UI.
- **Annual accounts PDF parsing failed.** `arsregnskap-leger-uten-grenser-norge-medecins-sans-frontieres-norway-2024.pdf` and 2023 parsed as binary via WebFetch. For programme-by-programme split (how much of the NOK 550m direct-purpose went to each OC / country / programme), offline PDF-to-text extraction is needed.
- **WebFetch blocked on legerutengrenser.no.** Most fetches to legerutengrenser.no returned 403 (bot protection). Data here is reconstructed from SNL, Wikipedia, Panoramanyheter, Innsamlingskontrollen, karrierestart, and targeted search snippets. Direct fetches from the site should be redone with proper User-Agent + rate limiting before production use.
- **Operating result 2024 flagged as negative.** Purehelp reports NOK -67.3m operating result on NOK 607.5m revenue (slightly different from Innsamlingskontrollen's 618.1m — the delta is likely accounting-category difference between regnskap and anskaffede midler). Equity NOK 146m positive. The negative operating result in 2024 is consistent with 2022 TV-aksjonen revenue being spent down through 2024 — not a structural deficit.
- **Founding year**. Brreg `stiftelsesdato` is 1996-11-18 (current legal entity). The Norwegian MSF section dates from **1996** (matches Brreg). MSF globally founded **1971-12-21** in Paris. App should display "Founded 1996 (Norway section); MSF globally 1971".
- **Association member count not public.** How many people sit on the association roll (field-worker alumni) isn't published. Board is known (9 members); voting membership is larger but unspecified.
- **State-funding share on ngo-landscape.md.** Listed as "~0" — confirmed as **0% by policy**. Momskompensasjon status needs separate verification; even if MSF claims momskomp (administered by Lottstift, routinely claimed by Innsamlingskontrollen-member orgs), that's a mechanical tax refund rather than a discretionary state grant, and doesn't contradict the policy.
- **TV-aksjonen 2022 classification.** The NOK 266 m raised through the 2022 TV-aksjonen is a public-broadcast fundraising drive, not a state grant. It's compatible with the no-state-funding policy even though the broadcast is NRK-coordinated.
