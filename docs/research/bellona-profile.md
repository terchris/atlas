# Bellona — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Miljøstiftelsen Bellona**, org.nr `948778599`. Bellona is Norway's flagship climate-and-industry think-and-do-tank: a stiftelse founded in 1986 that moved from direct-action protest in the late 1980s to become the country's most influential non-state actor on industrial decarbonisation, carbon capture, battery policy and (historically) Russian nuclear-safety work. It is the archetypal Norwegian "solution-oriented" environmental NGO, and also the one whose funding model most deviates from the donor-centric pattern used by the rest of the sector.

In our framework Bellona is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): professional operator with four international offices (Oslo, Brussels, Berlin, Vilnius — not Murmansk any more; see below), ~100 staff globally, no domestic chapters, no member democracy, no volunteer pathway. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces (Røde Kors, Sanitetskvinnene, Folkehjelp) doesn't apply, so Bellona doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It appears in the app in two places: **Compare NGOs** and the **Funding transparency** surface — and in both it's a deliberately sharp contrast to donor-funded humanitarians, because its revenue mix is fundamentally different.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, bellona.no, bellona.org, eu.bellona.org, SNL, Proff.no and Wikipedia. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

Bellona's self-description has shifted from "miljøstiftelse" to "teknologi- og løsningsorientert miljøorganisasjon". The work is organised around eleven programme areas; in practice four of them dominate the output (climate/energy, industrial decarbonisation, CCS, maritime):

| Programme | Norwegian | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **International climate & environment cooperation** | Internasjonalt klima- og miljøsamarbeid | EU policy work, UNFCCC processes |
| **Energy & electrification** | Energi og elektrifisering | Hydrogen, grid, renewable integration |
| **Carbon capture & storage (CCS)** | CO₂-fangst og lagring | The flagship — Bellona is Europe's most active NGO on CCS |
| **Industrial decarbonisation / green industry** | Grønn industri | Steel (Rana Gruber), aluminium (Hydro), mineral processing |
| **Maritime & aquaculture** | Maritim og havbruk | Ocean Forest, seaweed, shipping fuels |
| **Waste, pollution & circular economy** | Avfall, forurensning, sirkulær økonomi | District heating with CCS (Hafslund Celsio) |
| **Environmental protection & pollution response** | Miljøvern og forurensning | Industrial pollution monitoring, legal challenges |
| **Nuclear / Russia legacy** | Atomkraft | Cold-War nuclear waste, Rosatom monitoring (from Vilnius) |
| **Bioenergy & bioeconomy** | Bioenergi | Sustainable bio value chains |
| **Oil & gas** | Olje og gass | Critical engagement, not advocacy for |
| **Battery value chains** | Batteriverdikjeder | Morrow Batteries, mineral sourcing |

Source: bellona.no/om-bellona, cross-referenced bellona.org/about-bellona.

### Flagship initiatives

- **CCS advocacy (1999–present).** Bellona is the single NGO most responsible for the political existence of European CCS policy. The Mongstad "moon landing" 2006, the EU's CCS Directive, Longship (Northern Lights), and the 2024 EU Industrial Carbon Management Strategy all trace advocacy lineage to Bellona's framing. This is the work the rest of the sector either cheers or criticises — depending on stance.
- **Ocean Forest AS** — 50/50 joint venture with **Lerøy Seafood** (since 2013), growing kelp and mussels alongside salmon pens near Bergen; prototype for integrated multi-trophic aquaculture and ocean CO₂ capture.
- **Center for Marine Restoration (Lofoten, 2024).** New physical centre focused on restoring kelp forests along Northern Norway's coast (5 000 km² lost to sea-urchin grazing since the 1970s). Partners include Norrøna, IFF (International Flavors & Fragrances), Lerøy.
- **Nuclear safety / Russia legacy.** Alexander Nikitin's 1996–2000 espionage trial (for his Bellona report on the Northern Fleet) was the foundational geopolitical moment. Continued through **Bellona Environmental Transparency Center (ETC) in Vilnius (from 2022/2023)** — monitors Rosatom, nuclear waste in the Kola Peninsula, and the Komsomolets and Kursk submarine wrecks.
- **Battery value chains** — Bellona co-owns a stake in **Morrow Batteries**; published methodology work on green battery material sourcing.
- **Sahara Forest Project** — separate organisation Bellona helped start (deserts → food, freshwater, clean energy). Not core operational but part of the Bellona brand.

## Where they work

Four current offices + the Lofoten centre. **The Murmansk office is closed.** This is an important data point: Bellona spent 28 years (1994–2022) as one of the only Western NGOs with a permanent Russian presence, and that operation ended with the 2022 invasion.

| Office | Opened | Status | Staff (approx) | Focus |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **Oslo** (HQ) | 1986 | Active | ~40 | Norwegian policy, corporate cooperation, national campaigns |
| **Brussels** (Bellona Europa) | 1994 | Active | ~40 | EU climate/industrial policy — registered lobbyist on EU Transparency Register |
| **Berlin** (Bellona Deutschland) | 2021 | Active | ~15 | German industrial decarbonisation, CCS policy |
| **Vilnius** (Bellona Environmental Transparency Center) | 2022/2023 | Active | small team | Russian nuclear & environmental monitoring — successor to Murmansk/SPb |

| ~~Murmansk~~ | 1994 | **Closed 2022** (following Russian invasion of Ukraine) | — | — |

In April 2023 Russia declared Bellona an **"undesirable organisation"** — cooperation with or public mention of Bellona is now criminalised inside Russia. The Vilnius ETC team continues the monitoring work with staff relocated from the closed Russian offices.

## Money in — funding composition (THE distinctive point)

**This is the field where Bellona differs most from everything else in `ngo-landscape.md`.** It is **not** a donor-funded NGO. Using the 2023 figures Bellona itself publishes at bellona.no/bellonas-innteker:

| Source | Share 2023 | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| **Project support & grants** (CIFF, ClimateWorks, QCF, ECF, EU Horizon, NetZeroCities, Norges Forskningsråd, DSA, EEA/Norway Grants) | **63%** | Funds Brussels, Berlin, Vilnius/ETC and parts of Oslo |
| **Cooperation programme (næringslivssamarbeid — corporate partnerships)** | **~28%** | 30+ partners; all revenue goes to Oslo operations |
| **Basic operating grant (grunnstøtte, Klima- og miljødepartementet)** | **3%** | For admin in Oslo |
| **VAT compensation (momskompensasjon, Lotteri- og stiftelsestilsynet)** | **3%** | The **only** Lottstift flow Bellona receives |
| **Logo/support advertising** | **~2%** | ~75 advertisers, NOK 5 000–100 000 per agreement |
| **Support members & individual donations** | **~2%** | Small base, mostly AvtaleGiro + Vipps |
| **Lottstift grants (other)** | **~0%** | No lottery-based funding flows; `tilskudd.lottstift.no` will return nothing for 948778599 |

Source: bellona.no/bellonas-innteker; eu.bellona.org/funding (2021 data gave 25% cooperation programme, 63% project support — same structure).

### What this tells us — and why it matters for our app

The user's brief said "86%+ corporate funding" — the **live figures don't support that number**. The actual structure is:

- **~28% direct corporate money** (cooperation programme)
- **~2% corporate logo advertising**
- **~30% corporate-adjacent** all-in, which is still the highest corporate share of any major Norwegian NGO by a large margin

(For comparison: NRC has ~0% corporate; Røde Kors has small corporate partnership income; Leger Uten Grenser has a policy of **rejecting** corporate money since 2016. Bellona's 28–30% is an outlier in the Norwegian NGO landscape.)

The remaining ~63% is **also distinctive**: it is international climate philanthropy (CIFF, ClimateWorks, QCF, ECF), not Norwegian government development money. Bellona is closer in funding structure to a European climate policy shop (E3G, Climate Analytics, Agora Energiewende) than to a Norwegian humanitarian or membership NGO.

**The structural tension worth flagging on the Compare-NGOs page:** Bellona openly takes money from the same companies it is trying to influence. Named partners in 2025 include **Norsk Hydro** (aluminium — historically criticised by Bellona itself in the 1990s for mercury pollution), **Lerøy Seafood** (salmon aquaculture — contested sector), **Hafslund Celsio** (waste incineration + CCS), **Rana Gruber** (iron ore), **Eramet** (ferroalloys), **Jotun** (paint), **The Quartz Corp**, **Rare Earths Norway**, **Morrow Batteries**, **Grieg Seafood**, **ENRX**, **Elektroforeningen**, **Solar Norge**, **Eiendomsspar**, **Mestergruppen**, **Denofa/Pronofa**, **Brønnøy Kalk**, **Norsk Mineral**, **Ocean-Power** (from bellona.no/naeringsliv, 19 named + ~10 unnamed to reach "over 30").

Bellona's self-defence: the cooperation programme has an explicit **exclusion list** (oil companies; companies on the EU sanctions list), and the payments are flat fees not contingent on policy outcomes. Critics (SNL references these) say the arrangement still constitutes "bought and paid for by industry and state". The app should show this honestly — users comparing Bellona to Leger Uten Grenser's 100%-private-donor model need to see the contrast explicitly.

### Funding flows outside Lottstift

Like NRC, Bellona's real money does not flow through `tilskudd.lottstift.no` ordninger. The Lottstift-visible flow is momskompensasjon (~3%) only. Everything else is:
- **International philanthropy** (not in any Norwegian register)
- **EU Horizon / Commission contracts** (searchable via EU Financial Transparency System)
- **Norwegian state basic grant** via KLD (visible in Statsbudsjettet kap. 1400 miljøorganisasjoner)
- **Corporate invoices** under the cooperation programme (only visible in the annual accounts PDF)

A naive Lottstift query for 948778599 will show almost nothing — consistent with the NRC/KN/Folkehjelp pattern described in `ngo-landscape.md` §4.

## Money out — by programme and geography

Bellona's published total income 2023 was NOK ~63m. The organisation does **not** publish a programme-by-programme expenditure split on its public site. Rough allocation can be inferred from office location (since grant money follows office):

- **Oslo ~40%** — Norwegian policy work, cooperation programme (entirely Oslo-bound), campaigns
- **Brussels ~35%** — EU policy, CCS advocacy, industrial decarbonisation
- **Berlin ~15%** — German decarbonisation, EU-DE policy interface
- **Vilnius/ETC ~5%** — Russian nuclear monitoring
- **Lofoten centre + Ocean Forest** — small operational footprint, funded partly through JV/partner arrangements

For a verified split, the annual accounts PDF (deposited in Regnskapsregisteret) would be required. Bellona does not deposit accounts with Innsamlingskontrollen because it is not an IK member; the accounts are available via the Brreg Regnskapsregister API or Proff.no.

## How to engage

Bellona is **corporate-partnership-first, donation-second**. There is no member democracy, no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no school ambassador, no fadder. Verified pathways:

| Pathway | URL / route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Corporate cooperation programme** | `bellona.no/naeringsliv` | Primary engagement — for companies, not individuals |
| **Logo advertising (smaller businesses)** | `bellona.no` | NOK 5 000–100 000/year |
| **Monthly giver (AvtaleGiro)** | `bellona.profundo.no/bligiver` | Custom amount, recurring |
| **Vipps one-off** | Vipps "Bellona" or `12788` | QR code on site |
| **Bank transfer** | Account 6320 05 88888 | For larger one-off |
| **PayPal** | `paypal.com/paypalme/bellonano` | International |
| **Testamentarisk gave** | Via Jurio free will service | Bequest |
| **Minnegave** | Memorial gifts | Funeral context |
| **Spleis (crowdfunding)** | `spleis.no` | Birthday/event campaigns |
| **Newsletter / following** | Newsletter sign-up, LinkedIn, social media | Engagement-without-money pathway |
| **Tax deduction** | Gaves over 500 NOK qualify for skattefradrag | Norwegian rules |

Why no volunteer pathway? Three reasons:
1. **Operational model.** Bellona runs on technical expertise — engineers, lawyers, policy analysts. Work is professional-staff output, not volunteer-capacity-building.
2. **Stiftelse form.** Foundations don't have members; governance is the styre only.
3. **Corporate channel dominates.** Engagement capacity flows through the cooperation programme (B2B, not B2C). For individual engagement, Bellona's ask is "follow, read, donate" — not "do".

For the app, the Compare-NGOs card for Bellona should explicitly say "**ikke medlemsorganisasjon, ingen frivilligplassering — primærkanal er næringslivspartnerskap og støttemedlemskap**" rather than show an empty volunteer slot.

## Governance — the January 2026 transition

Bellona just went through its biggest governance change in its 40-year history:

- **Frederic Hauge** (co-founder 1986, public face for 40 years) stepped down as CEO and moved to **chair of the board** from January 5, 2026. Hauge was TIME "Hero of the Environment" 2007.
- **Sveinung Rotevatn** — former Klima- og miljøminister (V, 2020–2021), lawyer, Stortingsrepresentant — took over as **daglig leder for Bellona Norge** from January 5, 2026.
- **Nina Maria Hjellegjerde** continues as **CEO of the international organisation** (Bellona globally outside Norway).
- **Henrik A. Lund** (prior chair) continues as a board member.
- **Board**: Frederic Hauge (chair), Henrik A. Lund, Birgit Marie Liodden, Runa Amanda Skarbø, Igor Koudrik, plus employee representatives (Oskar Martin Linaker Njaa, Cathrine Ringstad, Benjamin Strandquist as alternate).
- **2018 leadership turnover**: SNL notes that in 2018 "the entire leadership departed due to disagreements with founder Hauge" — relevant governance context, though Bellona's public site does not address this directly.

### Governance documents

- Registered in **Stiftelsesregisteret** (supervised by Stiftelsestilsynet)
- Registered in **Frivillighetsregisteret**
- Registered in **EU Transparency Register** (Bellona Europa) as a declared lobbyist
- No published Code of Conduct / safeguarding policy on the current Norwegian site (contrast with NRC, which publishes these)

## Similar orgs — where does Bellona sit?

Bellona does not fit cleanly next to NRC/Flyktninghjelpen/SOS-barnebyer. The closest Norwegian peers by **model** (policy-oriented, industry-engaged, no chapters) and by **size**:

| Org | Model | 2023–24 income (NOK m) | Note |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **Bellona** | Industry-interface policy org | ~63 | Only NGO with this model at scale in Norway |
| Zero Emission Resource Organisation (ZERO) | Similar industry-interface | ~40–60 | Direct peer; also CCS-active; similar funding pattern |
| Naturvernforbundet | Membership-based advocacy | ~55 | Classic member NGO — opposite model |
| WWF Norge | International federation, donor-funded | ~100 | Donor-funded; programme-heavy |
| Regnskogfondet | Donor + state-funded | 328 | Norad/Klimainvest-dominated |
| Framtiden i våre hender | Member + grants | ~30 | Member-based; more activist |

**Bellona + ZERO** are the two Norwegian examples of the industry-interface policy model. Worth pairing in the Compare-NGOs page so users can see that Bellona isn't unique-but-weird — there's a recognisable category here.

## Caveats and gaps

- **2024 income not yet in public databases.** 2023 is the latest confirmed (~NOK 63.1m). The user brief estimates "~100m NOK income" — that is **higher than current public data suggests** for Miljøstiftelsen Bellona alone. Possible explanations: (a) rapid growth 2023→2024 from EU Horizon + CIFF/QCF grants, (b) inclusion of Bellona Europa (Brussels) and Bellona Deutschland (Berlin) as separate legal entities in the consolidated figure. The Norwegian entity alone was 63m in 2023. Verify against 2024 accounts when deposited (June 2025) or the 40-year-anniversary 2026 report.
- **Corporate share estimate ("86%+").** The user brief figure is **not supported by current public data.** Bellona's own income page shows cooperation programme = 28% and logo ads = 2–3% (combined ~30%). If "86% corporate" came from an older or differently-defined figure (e.g. including all project grants where the end-client is corporate, or a different year), the app copy should use **~28–30% direct corporate** and label the methodology. The point of the Compare-NGOs contrast still holds — 28% is already very high by Norwegian NGO standards — but the "86%" framing would be a factual error.
- **Murmansk office closed 2022.** The user brief lists Murmansk as a current office. It is not. The equivalent operation is **Vilnius (Bellona ETC)** from 2022/2023. Russia declared Bellona "undesirable" in April 2023.
- **Leadership change January 2026.** Any prior research that listed Frederic Hauge as CEO is now outdated. Current: Hauge = chair, Rotevatn = MD Norway, Hjellegjerde = CEO international.
- **Not an Innsamlingskontrollen member.** Bellona is not listed at innsamlingskontrollen.no. Ratios like "share-to-cause" or "innsamlingsprosent" are not calculated for it. Any Compare-NGOs row for Bellona should show these fields as "ikke IK-medlem" rather than empty.
- **Foundation capital small (NOK 100 000).** Compared to NRC's NOK 1 000 000. This is the legal minimum for an old stiftelse — not a meaningful financial data point, just a marker.
- **Næringskode mismatch.** Brreg lists Bellona under 94.992 "Aktiviteter i andre medlemsorganisasjoner ellers" — which is slightly misleading since Bellona isn't a membership organisation. This is a common Brreg classification issue for Norwegian miljøstiftelser.
- **Annual accounts not machine-parsed.** The activity account PDF from Regnskapsregisteret would give the exact programme/office expenditure split. Not extracted in this verification pass.
- **Havpanelet co-lead claim (user brief).** Could not be directly verified from bellona.no or bellona.org. The international "High Level Panel for a Sustainable Ocean Economy" is co-chaired by Norway and Palau at head-of-state level; Bellona is not a co-lead there. There may be a separate Norwegian-language "Havpanelet" or advisory role; flag for re-verification before putting on an app surface.
