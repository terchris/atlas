# SOS-barnebyer Norge — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Stiftelsen SOS-barnebyer Norge**, org.nr `947571958`. A mid-sized international child-welfare stiftelse, Oslo HQ, 49 employees, NOK 668m income 2024 — and, as of February 2026, in the middle of the **largest structural upheaval in its 62-year history**: severing ties with SOS Children's Villages International and phasing out the child-sponsorship (fadder) model that built the organisation.

In our framework SOS-barnebyer is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a donate-first international operator with no domestic chapters, no lokallag, no member democracy, and no volunteer pathway. Same structural slot as NRC, MSF Norge, Plan Norge, UNICEF-komiteen, CARE and Regnskogfondet. It appears in **Compare NGOs**, in the **Funding transparency** view, and — where relevant — in the **Global-context panel** during crises. It does **not** appear in Chapter Finder, Activity Atlas, Volunteer or Member pathways, or the activity-indicator matrix.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, sos-barnebyer.no, panoramanyheter.no, postkodelotteriet.no and SNL. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

SOS-barnebyer organises programme work around four broad areas. The taxonomy has shifted noticeably over the last two years as the organisation pivots from its traditional village-centric model toward locally-led family support. What's on `sos-barnebyer.no/vart-arbeid/` as of April 2026:

| Programme | Norwegian label | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Forebygging — family strengthening** | SOS-familieprogram | Counselling, food, health follow-up, education support and livelihoods for families in crisis so parents can continue caring for their children |
| **Beskyttelse og omsorg — alternative care** | SOS-familier / barneby | Stable care for children who have lost parental care — increasingly foster families and kinship care, historically SOS children's villages |
| **Nødhjelp — emergency response** | Nødhjelp | Rapid humanitarian response via the federation's local presence in 130+ countries; food, shelter, child-friendly spaces, psychosocial support |
| **Påvirkning — advocacy** | Påvirkning | Policy work domestically and internationally to strengthen child-rights protections and state care systems |

Source: sos-barnebyer.no/vart-arbeid/ (live, 2026-04-18).

### The strategic shift

Two programme changes matter for anyone profiling SOS-barnebyer right now:

1. **Child sponsorship (fadderskap) is being phased out.** Process started January 2024; by early 2026 nearly all of ~100 000 fadder were converted to general SOS-fadder (unrestricted). Stated reasons: data-protection law (can no longer share children's photos / personal details), digital safeguarding risk, programmatic shift toward prevention (most supported children live with their own families, not in SOS families), equity between sponsored and non-sponsored children, and operational flexibility. Attrition from conversion was <4%. (sos-barnebyer.no/nyheter/faser-ut-barnefadderskap, panoramanyheter.no)
2. **Village-centric operating model is being deprioritised.** Norway's only domestic SOS-barneby (Olsvik, Bergen) closed in 2018. International direction is toward family-based care and supporting existing local partner organisations ("lokalt ledet utvikling") rather than building and running SOS's own villages.

### Flagship concepts (in Norwegian public awareness)

- **SOS-barneby** — the historical village model: 10–15 family homes, each with a "SOS-mor" caring for 6–10 children without parental care. Still the dominant public image of the brand, even as operations shift away from it.
- **SOS-fadder / fadderskap** — the sponsor-a-child model Norway sold for 60 years. Being phased out in favour of general donor commitments.
- **SOS-familieprogram** — preventive family support. Self-reported outcomes from own site: 95% of children in the program have actively engaged parents, 82% are in school, 40% of families from the 2018 cohort are self-sufficient. (sos-barnebyer.no)

## Where they work

SOS Children's Villages International operates **in over 130 countries and territories with ~2 300 programs** (sos-barnebyer.no, April 2026). Self-reported country count has drifted: the 2022 annual report said 138, the current live page says "over 130". The federation is headquartered in Vienna; Norway's branch is one of ~30 national "promoting and supporting" associations that fundraise for and co-govern the global work.

Regional distribution (approximate, current site):

| Region | Countries |
|---|---|
| Africa | ~36 (Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia, South Africa, Nigeria, Ghana and others) |
| Europe | 20+ (incl. Ukraine, Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, Serbia, Nordic) |
| Asia & Oceania | 20+ (India, Nepal, Thailand, Cambodia, Philippines, etc.) |
| Americas | 22 (Central, South, and North America) |

**Norway's specific country focus**: the organisation doesn't publish a fixed list of "countries Norway funds". Traditional emphasis areas have included Bangladesh (Norway financed a complete village in 1979), various East African partners, and emergency contexts. The shift toward locally-led partnerships means the Norwegian branch is moving from "our village in X" language to "our partner in X" language.

**The break with SOS International** (announced 5 February 2026) adds a major caveat: SOS-barnebyer Norge has entered a two-year notice period to leave the international federation. Post-2028, the country-coverage picture for money flowing via Norway will be re-drawn — likely around a smaller set of strategic partner organisations chosen by Oslo, not the full 130+ federation footprint. See **Caveats**.

## Money in — funding composition

This is where SOS-barnebyer differs sharply from the operational humanitarians (NRC, Folkehjelp, Kirkens Nødhjelp). SOS-barnebyer is **overwhelmingly privately funded** — state money is a rounding error.

Own-site breakdown (sos-barnebyer.no/om-oss/ofte-stilte-sporsmal):

- **86%** of income goes to mission
- **13%** to fundraising
- **1%** to administration

(Innsamlingskontrollen's slightly different methodology gives 84.6% formålsprosent, 15.1% fundraising, 0.8% admin for 2024 — the own-site numbers round to the nearest percent and include slightly different cost categories.)

### What this tells us

- **SOS-barnebyer is a private-donor organisation**, not a state contractor. State grants via Lottstift are ~3% of income; there is no meaningful Norad framework agreement (unlike NRC, Folkehjelp, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Redd Barna). On the Jonas/Ola transparency axis, this is the cleanest "private giving from Norway" story in the sample — NRC's opposite.
- **Postkodelotteriet is the single biggest non-individual channel** at ~9% of income — a more significant share than for almost any other Norwegian NGO. SOS-barnebyer has been a Postkodelotteriet beneficiary since the Norwegian launch in 2018 (together with WWF Norge).
- **Income is declining** — 2022 NOK 755.8m → 2023 NOK 742.6m → 2024 NOK 668.2m (−12% over two years). The 2024 figure predates the fadder conversion completion. Whether the transition ultimately protects or damages revenue is a 2025–2026 question the next accounting cycle will answer.
- **State-funding share does not route outside Lottstift** in the way NRC/Folkehjelp/KN funding does. A naive Lottstift query for 947571958 gives a near-complete picture of public funding — unlike for operational humanitarians.

## How to engage

SOS-barnebyer is **donor-only** for the Norwegian public. No members, no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no school-ambassador programme exposed on the public site. The organisation explicitly states "Vi ansetter ingen fra Norge ved SOS-barnebyer i utlandet" — no Norwegian volunteers are deployed internationally either; all field staff are local hires.

### Why no volunteer pathway

The three structural reasons that apply to NRC apply equally here:

1. **Operational model.** SOS federation fieldwork uses local, trained, paid staff — SOS-mor roles are long-term employment, not volunteer placements. Untrained Norwegian volunteers don't fit.
2. **Stiftelse form.** Foundations don't have members; governance is the styre + kontrollkomite only. Volunteer involvement would need a separate organisational structure.
3. **Public-facing engagement is financial.** The organisation optimises its public UX around converting awareness into monthly giving. Physical volunteering doesn't factor.

For the app, SOS-barnebyer's Compare-NGOs card should explicitly say "donasjonsbasert organisasjon — ingen lokallag, ingen frivilligplassering i Norge, ingen utsending av norske frivillige til utlandet" rather than show empty volunteer/member slots.

## Governance

- **Form**: Stiftelse, regulated under stiftelsesloven; supervised by Stiftelsestilsynet.
- **Generalsekretær**: **Sissel Aarak** (current; succeeded Bente Lier who preceded her).
- **Styreleder**: **Frode Hvattum** (uavhengig næringsliv; strategisk rådgiver i Skift; 20 års erfaring fra Accenture, Ruter, Freyr).
- **Board**: 8 members, including one employee representative (Cicilie Romundstad Asbøll). Other members named on site: TC Høiseth, Eskil Pedersen, Cathrine Kaasen Conradi, Grethe Bergly. Board meets 7–8 times per year, appoints the generalsekretær.
- **Senior management** (Oslo HQ, April 2026): Hien Doan (COO), Trine Riis Hansen (CPO — programmes), Sigurd Skjefstad (CMO — marketing).
- **Kontrollkomite** (oversight committee): Ingjerd Schou (chair), Gunvor Ulstein, Helge Leiro Baastad.

### International governance (changing)

Until February 2026, SOS-barnebyer Norge was part of **SOS Children's Villages International**, headquartered in Vienna — a federation of ~30 national promoting/supporting associations plus program countries. On **5 February 2026** the Norwegian board, under then-new leadership, announced it would leave the federation over a two-year notice period. Stated reasons per panoramanyheter: outdated federation structures (60–70 years old), high administrative overhead, disagreement over the child-sponsorship model, federation IT/operational systems locked to the village-and-fadder model, and legacy controversies around founder Hermann Gmeiner. The international board chair (Domenico Parisi) expressed public concern that the split will weaken collective advocacy and confuse donors.

Practical implication for anyone using this profile: **between now and early 2028, SOS-barnebyer Norge's international footprint, programme portfolio, and possibly legal name will be in motion.** Re-verify before any production surface treats "SOS-barnebyer Norge operates in 130+ countries" as a stable claim.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Organisation in flux.** The February 2026 decision to leave SOS Children's Villages International, combined with the fadder phase-out completed in 2024–2025, makes any claim beyond Brreg legal facts a moving target until at least early 2028. The country-count, programme taxonomy, brand name, and donor-mix percentages may all change within the profile's normal refresh window.
- **Share-to-cause methodology drift.** `ngo-landscape.md` cites 84.5%; live Innsamlingskontrollen page shows 84.6%; own-site FAQ says 86%. All three are defensible with different cost bucketing; app UX should pick one source (recommend Innsamlingskontrollen) and label it.
- **Country count drift.** Self-reported over 138 (2022 report), "over 130" (current site), 130 (FAQ). Use "130+" as the conservative number; rerun verification if a precise figure is needed on a user-facing surface.
- **Givers number is pre-conversion.** "~100 000 givere" is the figure from before the 2024–2025 fadder-conversion campaign. Post-conversion attrition was <4%; net number is plausibly higher after new recruitment, but has not been re-stated publicly on the live site. Treat as "order of magnitude 100 000" rather than a current point estimate.
- **Annual accounts PDF not parsed.** The 2024 årsregnskap PDF on Innsamlingskontrollen wasn't extracted in this pass — programme-by-programme money-out split and country-level expenditure exist inside but need headless/PDF-to-text extraction. Worth running offline once for Tier C detail page.
- **Lottstift live page JS-rendered.** `tilskudd.lottstift.no/mottaker/947571958` returns an empty shell to simple WebFetch. The NOK 20.8m figure is from `ngo-landscape.md` §4.1 (older Google-snippet cached totals). Exact 2024 breakdown (momskomp vs Norsk Tipping vs department grants) needs headless fetch.
- **Postkodelotteriet 2024 amount.** NOK 60.4m is from news-search snippets of Postkodelotteriet's own reporting; the own-site page for SOS-barnebyer is narrative-only and doesn't list per-year amounts. Re-verify against Postkodelotteriet's annual report before publishing a single-year number.
- **Founding-year ambiguity.** Organisational founding 1964 (Norwegian branch, foreningsform); stiftelse 1983 (current legal entity); Brreg Enhetsregister entry 1995. App should display "Founded 1964, stiftelse 1983".
- **Federation membership unresolved.** As of 2026-04-18, SOS-barnebyer Norge is still *in* SOS Children's Villages International during the two-year notice period. Any statement about being "part of" vs "previously part of" the federation has a sell-by date around February 2028.
