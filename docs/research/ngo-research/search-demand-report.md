# What people in Norway search for — and what NGOs call it

**Validating the cross-NGO activity taxonomy against Google search demand.**
4 October 2026. Google Ads Keyword Planner, location Norway, Google Search, September 2025 – August
2026, five rounds, 1 418 distinct search phrases. Raw exports and every derived file are in
[`keyword-planner/`](keyword-planner/README.md).

## Summary

1. **People search for their problem, not for a service.** *ensomhet* (loneliness) is searched
   1 000–10 000 times a month; the services that answer it — *besøkstjeneste*, *besøksvenn* — 10–1 000.
2. **The plain or official word wins; NGO programme names lose.** *fri rettshjelp* (1K–10K) beats
   *gratis rettshjelp*; Nasjonalforeningen's *Gå med oss*, *Spis med oss* and *Strikk med oss* are
   searched 0–10 times a month each, while the same activities as *trening for eldre*, *treffpunkt*
   and *strikkekafe* are searched 100–1 000 times.
3. **There are two searchers.** One needs help (*gratis advokat*, *gratis mat*), one wants to give it
   (*frivillig arbeid*). The second searches through the organisation's name — *frivillig røde
   kors* 100–1K — not through the activity (*frivillig leksehjelp* 10–100).
4. **Long, combined labels are never searched.** *Besøksvenn og besøkstjeneste*, *Språkkafé og
   norsktrening*, *Møteplass og kafé*: 0–10 each. A label people should find must be one word they type.
5. **For health, violence and dementia, Google hides the numbers.** *demens*, *voldsutsatt*,
   *menneskehandel* return 0. That is advertising policy, not a lack of need.

A search that only matches service names misses the people the service exists for. The need
words have to be part of what a category is found by.

⚠️ A name's volume only means something when the name is distinctive. Kirkens Bymisjon's *Byparken*,
*Sporet* and *Enga* score high because they are also place names and everyday words — those
searchers are not looking for the NGO.

## 3. Two searchers: the one who needs help and the one who wants to help

| Phrase | Searches / month |
|---|---:|
| frivillig arbeid | 100–1K |
| frivillig røde kors | 100–1K |
| bli fosterhjem | 100–1K |
| bli frivillig | 10–100 |
| frivillig besøksvenn | 10–100 |
| frivillig leksehjelp | 10–100 |

Volunteers search for *volunteering* and for *organisations*, rarely for a specific activity. A
search service has to answer both: "where can I get help with X" and "where can I volunteer" — and
must match NGO names, not only categories.

## 4. Where the demand is

Largest searched words by activity area (generic terms, other meanings excluded):

| Searches / month | Words |
|---|---|
| 10K–100K | bruktbutikk |
| 1K–10K | førstehjelpskurs, krisesenter, speider, gudstjeneste, åpen barnehage, fri rettshjelp, ensomhet, flyktninger, asylmottak, kirkens sos |
| 100–1K | språkkafe, leksehjelp, besøksvenn, treffpunkt, aktiviteter for eldre, fritidsklubb, sommerleir, likeperson, trening for eldre, gratis mat, hospits, arbeidstrening nav, hjelpetelefon, vold i nære relasjoner, strikkekafe, leteaksjon |
| 10–100 | most programme and service names |

Five of the forty draft categories have no search demand under any phrasing tried — first-aid
standby, crisis preparedness groups, reading friend, witness support, political advocacy. They stay
in the taxonomy for classification, but they are not doors people walk through.

## 5. What Google will not tell

Searches about health, violence, addiction and dementia return 0 or are removed: *demens*,
*voldsutsatt*, *menneskehandel*, *helsehjelp papirløse*. Google Ads restricts advertising on
sensitive topics and hides the figures with it. For these areas a low number means
**"Google won't say"**, not "nobody searches". They are marked `sensitive` in the taxonomy and must
be judged on other evidence.

## What this means

**For the NGOs.** The names NGOs give their programmes are, for the most part, not the words people
use. Describing each activity with the shared category and its searched words — *språkkafe*,
*treffpunkt*, *trening for eldre* — next to the programme name makes it findable. That is the case
for adopting the shared taxonomy: it is what people already type.

**For Atlas search.** Index three kinds of word per activity: the category's search term, the need
people describe (*ensomhet*, *gratis advokat*), and the NGO's own name. Answer both searchers —
help-seekers and volunteers. Keep long display labels for reading, never for matching.

**For the taxonomy.** Taxonomy v2 ([`categories-v2.csv`](categories-v2.csv)) applies all of this:
38 categories, each with a short display label, a validated primary search term, need terms and
volunteer terms; 29 are search entry points, 5 are classification-only, 4 are `sensitive`; two
draft categories with no demand were merged away.

## Limits

- **Bands, not counts.** The account has no ad spend, so Google gives volumes as bands (0–10,
  10–100, 100–1K, 1K–10K, 10K–100K). Use them to rank, never to add up. Exact figures need an
  account with active spend.
- **Google only.** No other search engine, no on-site search, no app stores.
- **National.** Local demand (one kommune, one city) is not measured here.
- **Other meanings.** A word's volume includes every meaning: *matkasse* is mostly meal kits,
  *natteravn* also a bird, *turvenn* an app. Such terms are flagged and kept out of the rankings.
- **Sensitive topics are hidden** (section 5).
