# CARE Norge — organisation profile

Reference profile for **CARE Norge Stiftelse**, org.nr `995722186`. CARE Norge is the Norwegian member of the CARE International confederation and the most focused gender-lens humanitarian/development actor in the Norwegian NGO landscape — their entire mandate is "bekjemp all urett mot kvinner og jenter" (combat all injustice against women and girls).

In our framework CARE Norge is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a small professional stiftelse with no domestic chapter network, no member democracy, and no volunteer pathway. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces doesn't apply, so CARE Norge doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It does still appear in three places in the app — Compare NGOs, Funding transparency, and the Global-context panel during active crises — and this profile is the data source behind those surfaces.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, care.no, snl.no and Wikipedia. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

CARE Norge organises its public-facing work around **five focus areas** — four thematic pillars covering the life-stages of a woman's autonomy plus a cross-cutting emergency response pillar. The taxonomy is distinctly Norwegian (the English CARE confederation pages use a different labelling) but maps cleanly onto CARE International's global "Gender in Emergencies" and Women's Economic Empowerment frameworks.

| Pillar (no) | Pillar (en) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Hennes trygghet** | Her safety | Protection from gender-based violence, child marriage prevention, safe spaces in crisis contexts |
| **Hennes kropp** | Her body | Sexual and reproductive health and rights (SRHR), maternal health, bodily autonomy |
| **Hennes penger** | Her money | Economic empowerment, savings and loan groups (VSLA), value-chain integration, financial inclusion |
| **Hennes stemme** | Her voice | Political and civic participation, women's leadership, engaging men and boys for gender equality |
| **Nødhjelp** | Emergency aid | Rapid humanitarian response with explicit gender lens — food, water, shelter, protection, SRHR in crisis |

Source: care.no/om-arbeidet-vart (live, 2026-04-18); cross-referenced with care.no/om-care.

### Flagship initiatives

- **Landsbyspareklubb / spare- og lånegrupper (VSLA — Village Savings and Loan Associations)**. CARE's single most distinctive global contribution to development practice. The model was developed in Niger in **1991 by Norwegian CARE employee Moira Eknes** — a founding-story CARE Norge anchors heavily. Structure: 15–25 women save a fixed shared amount, set their own rules and interest rates, and lend to each other for enterprise, school fees, healthcare, seeds or equipment. Training wraps literacy, numeracy, financial management, health, SRHR and political leadership around the capital cycle. **Over 20 million women** globally have moved through VSLA groups since 1991. CARE Norge specifically runs partnerships with Kenya Commercial Bank in Burundi and eight microfinance institutions in Rwanda reaching ~300 000 group members. Moira Eknes received the "Livslang innsats" (lifetime achievement) award on NRK's *Norske helter* in 2016.
- **Women Lead in Emergencies**. Global CARE model (piloted from 2018) operationalising women's leadership in humanitarian crises — Mali, Niger, Uganda, Colombia, Philippines, Tonga. Shifts power and resources directly to women's groups in conflict and disaster settings. In 2020, Women Lead worked with 804 women's groups. Part of CARE's "Gender in Emergencies" doctrine.
- **TV-aksjonen partnerships** — CARE Norge has been the beneficiary **twice**: 2009 (NOK **194 million** raised, spent across ten countries) and 2019 (NOK **240 million** raised, targeting 400 000 women in vulnerable areas). CARE is one of relatively few organisations to have hosted TV-aksjonen twice; the 2019 edition financed a multi-year scale-up of VSLA and SRHR programming that runs through 2026.

This list is 12 entries but CARE Norge's own communications say "11 countries" — the exact composition shifts year-to-year depending on active programme portfolio; Afghanistan and Myanmar in particular have been staff-presence-only in some recent periods.

## Money in — funding composition

CARE Norge is a **heavily institutional-funded operator** — the same pattern as NRC and Kirkens Nødhjelp but at a tenth the scale. Its 2024 income of NOK 227m is overwhelmingly project grants from Nordic and EU institutional donors, supplemented by private individual giving, a flagship Postkodelotteriet partnership, and corporate partners.

### Funding sources (2024, not publicly broken down by share)

From CARE Norge's own description:

| Category | Donors |
|---|---|
| **Norwegian state** | Utenriksdepartementet (UD), Norad, Innovasjon Norge |
| **Nordic sister states** | Sida (Sweden), Danida (Denmark) |
| **EU institutions** | ECHO (humanitarian aid), INTPA (international partnerships) |
| **Education/research** | HK-dir / DIKU |
| **Lottery / foundations** | **Postkodelotteriet (Norway)**, Svenska Postkodestiftelsen, Grieg Foundation |
| **Corporate partners** | Finansforbundet, Komplett.no, named næringslivspartnere |
| **Private individuals** | AvtaleGiro månedsgivere, one-off donations, Vipps, SMS, minnegaver, testamentariske gaver |

### What this tells us

- **Norwegian state share (UD + Norad + Innovasjon Norge) is material but CARE Norge doesn't disclose the %.** `ngo-landscape.md` treats UD/Norad as the anchor bucket. 2024 annual accounts PDF (not text-parseable in this verification pass) contains the exact split in Note 2 of Årsregnskap-2024.pdf.
- **Postkodelotteriet is a named, public partnership.** CARE Norge is one of the 23 ideelle organisations in Norway's Postkodelotteriet AS cohort (org.nr tied to the Lotteritilsynet permission that allows up to NOK 410m/year in turnover across all 23). Exact annual distribution to CARE Norge is not published on the lottery's org page but is disclosed in CARE Norge's own 2024 accounts. Cross-Nordic dimension: the **Svenska Postkodestiftelsen** (Swedish sister) is listed separately as a multi-project funder alongside the Norwegian lottery.
- **Income dropped sharply 2023 → 2024** (NOK 276m → 227m, a **–18%** swing). This is not a trend to ignore — the 2024 year shows **expense exceeding income** (NOK 239.9m costs vs 227.0m income = ~NOK 13m deficit), likely financed by reserve drawdown as some multi-year 2023 grants completed. Worth flagging in any transparency surface.
- **No Norwegian state funding via Lottstift.** Like other operational humanitarians (NRC, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Norsk Folkehjelp for the Norad share, Plan, Redd Barna), CARE Norge's Norwegian state funding routes through **direct UD/Norad project agreements and rammeavtaler**, not through `tilskudd.lottstift.no` ordninger. A naive Lottstift query for 995722186 returns effectively nothing — the real money is in Statsbudsjettet kap. 151–160 (UD humanitarian and development) and Norad prosjektavtaler. See `ngo-landscape.md` §4 caveats.

### Surprising finding

The 2024 numbers suggest CARE Norge is running **slightly over breakeven in deficit**: direct purpose costs 232.1m + fundraising 4.2m + admin 3.6m = 239.9m, vs income 227.0m. This is a **–12.9m operating deficit** on the 2024 activity account — sharply different from the "95% to cause" narrative, which is true on a cost-mix basis but masks the fact that 2024 spent more than it raised. This is typical stiftelse accounting when multi-year grants carried over from 2023 (which was unusually strong at 276m) finished their disbursement cycles in 2024. The 2025 income will be the real indicator of whether this is a one-off or a compression.

## How to engage

CARE Norge is **donor-only** for the Norwegian public. There is no member, no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no school-ambassador or student-ambassador programme on the public site.

Verified pathways (care.no/stott-arbeidet-vart):

| Pathway | Route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Månedsgiver (fast giver, AvtaleGiro) | `care.no/stott-arbeidet-vart` — flexible amount (150/200/250/350 kr typical) | Primary ask — no "fadder" framing; CARE uses "månedsgiver" |
| One-off donation (Vipps) | Vipps code **2468** | |
| One-off donation (card) | `care.no/stott-arbeidet-vart/muligheter` | |
| SMS giving | Text **CARE to 1919** (250 NOK) | |
| Bank transfer — general | 8200 01 40046 | |
| Bank transfer — emergency | 1644 10 50082 | Earmarked nødhjelp account |
| Gave med mening (occasion gifts) | care.no webshop | Birthdays, weddings |
| Minnegave (memorial gift) | care.no link | Funeral-context |
| Testamentarisk gave (bequest) | care.no link | |
| Corporate partnership | care.no/naeringsliv | Næringslivspartner / støttespiller |
| Tax deduction | Norwegian skattefradrag rules (500–25 000 NOK) | |

**Notable gap vs sibling orgs**: CARE Norge does **not** use the "fadder" (sponsor-a-child/woman) model despite the gender focus lending itself to it. Plan International Norge (~115 000 fadder) and SOS-barnebyer (~100 000 givere) use sponsor framing; CARE Norge chose the neutral "månedsgiver" framing, consistent with CARE's feminist-empowerment positioning that avoids paternalist framings. Worth surfacing in Compare-NGOs.

Why no volunteer pathway?

1. **Stiftelse form.** Foundations don't have members; the only governance bodies are the styre. Volunteer involvement would require a separate structure.
2. **Operational model.** CARE Norge is an implementation partner for CARE International's country offices — field operations are staffed by national CARE country-office employees, not Norwegian volunteers.
3. **Small domestic footprint.** 23 employees in Oslo. There isn't a programme surface that can absorb volunteer time in a structured way.

For the app, CARE Norge's card in the Compare-NGOs view should explicitly say "donasjonsbasert organisasjon — ingen lokallag, ingen frivilligplassering" rather than show an empty volunteer slot.

## Thematic overlap — CARE Norge, N.K.S., and Plan

CARE Norge is one of three Norwegian NGOs whose public mandate is explicitly about women and girls. The three occupy **distinct but adjacent** thematic slots that the Compare-NGOs surface should make legible, not collapse:

| Org | Tier | Geography | Women/girls angle | Engagement model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Norske Kvinners Sanitetsforening (N.K.S.)** | A (550 lokalforeninger) | **Domestic** (Norway) | Women's health — forskning på kvinnesykdommer, voldsutsatte kvinner, krisesentre, integrering | Member + volunteer + donate |
| **CARE Norge** | C (stiftelse) | **Global** (11 focus countries, 100+ via confederation) | Women's empowerment — economic autonomy, SRHR, leadership, GBV in crisis | Donate only |
| **Plan International Norge** | C (stiftelse) | **Global** (60+ countries) | Girls' rights — education, child protection, child marriage, youth voice | Fadder + donate |

For app implementation, this means:
- **Compare-NGOs** should surface all three when a user's signal is "women/girls" — with a clear label that N.K.S. is domestic, CARE is global-women, Plan is global-girls.
- **Activity-indicator matrix** (kommune-level) only applies to N.K.S.; CARE and Plan don't have kommune-facing activities.
- **Thematic clustering** is a UX dimension orthogonal to the Tier A/B/C structural clustering. The "cause-and-impact" surface in Tier C orgs should show N.K.S. as a domestic counterpart to CARE's global lens, same way NRC's surface can show Røde Kors flyktningsguide as a domestic counterpart to NRC's international refugee mandate.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Share-to-cause discrepancy**. Four defensible figures: 95% (CARE's general claim), 92% (some 2024-specific CARE communications), 95.4% (ngo-landscape baseline), 96.7% (computed from Innsamlingskontrollen 2024 costs). Pick one and label methodology — recommend 95% as the rounded, five-year-stable number.
- **2024 deficit year**. Income 227m < total costs 239.9m (–12.9m). This is a real operational signal but not surfaced on care.no. Worth flagging in transparency UX; requires checking whether 2025 income recovered.
- **Income drop 2023 → 2024** was –18% (276m → 227m). Largest driver is likely the tail-off of TV-aksjonen-2019-funded multi-year programmes and the closing of specific UD/Norad project cycles. Verify against Årsberetning-2024.pdf once PDF parsing available.
- **Exact funder split not publicly disclosed on website**. CARE Norge lists donor *categories* (UD, Norad, SIDA, DANIDA, EU, private, etc.) but not the NOK or % split. The 2024 annual accounts PDF contains it in Note 2 but the PDF is not text-parseable via WebFetch (same limitation as NRC annual accounts).
- **Staff count variance**. Brreg `antallAnsatte` is 23 (point-in-time). CARE Norge 2024 self-report is 27 FTE / årsverk. The ansatte-og-styre page lists ~32 named employees including directors. All three numbers are defensible for different definitions (registered / FTE / named persons). `ngo-landscape.md` uses 23 from Brreg; should add a footnote about the 27 årsverk figure.
- **Country list ambiguity**. CARE Norge says "11 countries" but Wikipedia lists 12 (Afghanistan, Myanmar, Burundi, DR Congo, Mali, Niger, Rwanda, Syria, Yemen, Jordan, Tanzania, Uganda). The composition shifts year to year depending on programme portfolio; exact 2024 list needs verification from Aktivitetsrapport-2024.pdf.
- **Founding-year ambiguity**. 1980 (organisational founding as Norwegian CARE International member, Torkild Skallerud first director) vs 2010-06-17 (current stiftelse legal entity). App should display "Founded 1980" with a footnote about the 2010 stiftelse re-registration — same pattern as NRC's 1946/1997 split.
- **Postkodelotteriet annual amount not public**. Partnership is confirmed and ongoing but the NOK distribution to CARE Norge per year is only in the 2024 accounts PDF, not on the lottery's own org page.
- **Board roster minor gap**. Full board verified (6 external + 1 ansattvalgt + 1 vara). Background/employment of individual board members not captured in this pass — would need follow-up fetches to get sector backgrounds for the transparency surface.
