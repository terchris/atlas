# Kirkens Nødhjelp — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Kirkens Nødhjelp / Norwegian Church Aid (NCA)**, org.nr `951434353`. NCA is Norway's second-largest faith-based humanitarian (after Frelsesarmeen on domestic social work; NCA is the largest on international aid) with NOK 1.379 bn income in 2024 and an unusually hybrid engagement model that routes fundraising and volunteer mobilisation through **Den norske kirkes menigheter** rather than a chapter network of its own.

In our framework NCA is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a professional humanitarian operator with no domestic chapters, no member democracy on the NCA legal entity, and a donate-first public pathway. But it's the Tier C case that deserves an asterisk — the annual **Fasteaksjonen** is Norway's largest single-event volunteer mobilisation (≈40 000 collectors, ≈1 200 congregations) and it happens entirely through a different institution (Den norske kirke + frikirker). That pattern matters for how the Compare-NGOs surface labels NCA, and it's the surprising finding this profile exists to flag.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, kirkensnodhjelp.no, fasteaksjonen.no, and cross-referenced via regjeringen.no, Store norske leksikon, Wikipedia, and ACT Alliance. Caveats at the bottom.

## What they do

NCA programmes cluster around six thematic areas, the same taxonomy used in its Norad framework agreement and in its English-language programme pages:

| Programme | Norwegian | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH)** | Vann, sanitær og hygiene | NCA's largest and most recognisable programme; climate-resilient WASH in 19+ countries. Fasteaksjonen has centred WASH for most of the last decade |
| **Climate Resilience and Faith-Based Climate Action** | Klimaresiliens | Theological framing + practical adaptation; NCA links faith actors to national climate policy via ACT Alliance's 2023–2026 Climate Advocacy Framework |
| **Gender-Based Violence Prevention** | Kjønnsbasert vold | GBV response and prevention, often paired with WASH in displacement settings |
| **Peace-building and Conflict Transformation** | Fredsbygging | Religious-actor dialogue, conflict-transformation work; NCA has leveraged religious networks for peace in South Sudan, Mali, Colombia |
| **Humanitarian Emergency Response** | Nødhjelp | ACT Alliance Rapid Response mechanism; active in Gaza, Sudan, Ukraine, Syria, DR Congo |
| **Economic Justice / Advocacy for Global Justice** | Økonomisk rettferdighet, rettferdig skatt | Tax justice, debt cancellation (NCA was a key actor in Norway's 2006 cancellation of debt for Ecuador, Egypt, Jamaica, Peru, Sierra Leone), anti-corruption |

Source: kirkensnodhjelp.no/en/how-we-work; regjeringen.no (Norad framework announcement April 2020).

### Flagship initiatives

- **Fasteaksjonen** — annual Lenten door-knocking campaign (the Tuesday before Palm Sunday). See dedicated section below. 2025 edition raised **NOK 36 m**; cumulative 50+ year total **NOK 1.75 bn**.
- **Ledsagerprogrammet** — NCA runs Norway's participation in the ecumenical accompaniment programme in Palestine/Israel (EAPPI). Small but symbolically important; a rare NCA pathway that does involve Norwegian individuals on the ground.
- **Changemaker** — NCA's youth organisation (own org identity, staffed out of NCA, elected leader sits on NCA styret). Changemaker is Norway's youth voice on global-justice issues (tax, climate, debt).
- **Resultatrapport** — NCA's annual results publication; pairs with the årsregnskap as the narrative disclosure.

## Money in — funding composition

This is where NCA diverges most sharply from Flyktninghjelpen/NRC and the Tier C template. NCA's income is **~63% public grants**, **~18% privately collected funds**, **~18% other grants** (which includes international church and foundation donors) — so Norwegian state money is dominant, and the biggest single line is a Norad framework agreement that **bypasses Lottstift entirely**.

### 2024 income composition

| Source | NOK m | Share |
|---:|---|---:|
| Public grants (offentlige tilskudd) | 872.8 | 63.3% |
| Other grants (andre tilskudd — inc. international donors, church networks, foundations) | 242.9 | 17.6% |
| Privately collected funds (innsamlede midler) | 241.3 | 17.5% |
| Other income | 22.5 | 1.6% |
| **Total** | **1 379.5** | **100.0%** |

Source: [Innsamlingskontrollen — Kirkens Nødhjelp](https://www.innsamlingskontrollen.no/organisasjoner/kirkens-nodhjelp-norwegian-church-aid/) (2024 one-pager).

### The Norad framework — the key insight

The single largest funding stream inside those 872.8 m public grants is NCA's **Norad rammeavtale**, signed 2 April 2020 for five years (renewed/extended thereafter). Key facts:

- **~NOK 350 m per year** — explicitly described at signing as the largest single agreement Norad had ever concluded with a humanitarian NGO.
- Covers **19 countries**.
- Four thematic pillars: WASH, GBV, peace-building, strengthening civil society + religious-minority rights advocacy.
- Routes through Statsbudsjettet kap. 160/170 (sivilt samfunn) — **does not appear in `tilskudd.lottstift.no`** because it's not a lotto-funded ordning.

On top of that, NCA receives **UD humanitarian allocations** (kap. 150 humanitær bistand) for acute responses in Ukraine, Gaza, Sudan, and DRC, plus regional/country allocations from Norwegian embassies. Those also flow outside Lottstift.

The practical implication for our app: a naive query of `tilskudd.lottstift.no` for org.nr 951434353 returns near-nothing. The real state-money trail is in Norad's annual tildelinger + UD/MFA humanitarian allocations, neither of which has a public JSON API today. `ngo-landscape.md` §4.3 already flags this pattern — NCA is the textbook example.

### Private giving — the menighet-routed part

NCA's own English finance page says the org receives about **NOK 161 m/year from private donors** on a steady-state basis (a figure that's grown in 2024 to NOK 241 m including church-collection inflows). Of that:

- **~NOK 30–36 m** is Fasteaksjonen (varies year-to-year; 2025 was NOK 36 m, a record).
- The balance is recurring givere (AvtaleGiro, fadderskap for WASH/Endre project), corporate partnerships, bequests (testamentariske gaver), and channel-specific campaigns.

The structural oddity: a meaningful share of NCA's private income arrives pre-aggregated through **menighetsregnskap** — local Den norske kirke congregations collect (often via kollekt + Fasteaksjonen) and remit to NCA. Individual donors in these flows are never NCA "members" in any registrable sense; NCA has no direct CRM relationship with the >30 000 people who hand cash to a konfirmant at their door. That's unusual in the Norwegian NGO landscape and structurally distinct from NRC's donor database or Røde Kors's member roster.

## The Fasteaksjonen phenomenon

**Bottom line**: Norway's single largest non-governmental volunteer mobilisation each year happens at NCA's request, but is executed entirely by an institution NCA does not control — Den norske kirke + frikirkene.

### The numbers (2025 edition)

- **~40 000 collectors** (bøssebærere), predominantly ~14-15-year-old konfirmanter from the year's confirmation cohort
- **~1 200 menigheter** (congregations) participating
- **NOK 36 m** collected (record year; combines door-to-door + digital sponsor collection)
- **Timing**: always Tuesday before Palm Sunday (the week before Easter), with sponsor-collection running 2 weeks before that
- **Cumulative 50+ year history**: **NOK 1.75 bn** raised

Source: [Om fasteaksjonen — fasteaksjonen.no](https://www.fasteaksjonen.no/om-fasteaksjonen/).

### Why this is structurally unusual

Fasteaksjonen is Norway's **second-largest nasjonal pengeinnsamling after TV-aksjonen**, but unlike TV-aksjonen (which rotates recipient NGOs annually and is operated by NRK + Innsamlingsrådet), Fasteaksjonen routes to NCA every single year. It's effectively a **permanent piggy-back on the Den norske kirke konfirmasjon pipeline**:

1. ~55–60% of Norwegian 14-year-olds confirm in Den norske kirke each year (~40 000 of ~60 000 birth cohort).
2. DNK uses Fasteaksjonen as a **standard konfirmasjonsundervisning module** — konfirmanter learn about global-justice themes, then go door-to-door.
3. Menigheten pays in to NCA.
4. NCA supplies curriculum (konfirmantmateriell), digital infrastructure (sponsor collection via Spleis), bøsser, and the annual campaign theme.

In terms of our framework, this means:

- **NCA is Tier C** on its own legal entity (no chapters, no members) — ✓
- **NCA has chapter-like surface area** via 1 200 menigheter that it does not own, cannot direct operationally, and whose volunteer pipeline it taps once per year — ✗ for the chapter-model rendering
- Comparing NCA's "volunteer count" to Røde Kors's 42 000 is **analytically defensible on paper** (both ≈40 000) but **operationally misleading** — Røde Kors volunteers serve year-round across activity areas; NCA Fasteaksjonen volunteers serve one evening

For the Compare-NGOs surface, the right label is **"Donasjonsbasert — mobiliserer årlig ~40 000 frivillige via Den norske kirkes menigheter (Fasteaksjonen), men har ingen egne lokallag"**. That's honest in both directions.

## How to engage

NCA is donor-first but the menighet route is first-class, not an afterthought.

| Pathway | URL / route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring giver (AvtaleGiro / Vipps) | kirkensnodhjelp.no/gi-stotte | Primary ask — fadderskap on WASH/Endre project |
| One-off donation | Vipps 2426 (Fasteaksjonen) or Vipps 8888 (general) | |
| Fasteaksjonen konfirmant | Via local menighet — not through NCA directly | Annual; the main non-professional engagement route |
| Fasteaksjonen sponsor | fasteaksjonen.no/digital-innsamling | Digital sponsor collection; anyone can set up a profile |
| Ledsagerprogrammet (EAPPI) | kirkensnodhjelp.no/en/how-we-work | Palestine/Israel accompaniment — requires application, training, 3-month posting |
| Changemaker (youth org) | changemaker.no | 13–30 år, own membership structure, elected leadership — this is the closest thing to a chapter network, but it's formally distinct from NCA proper |
| Webshop — gaver med mening | gaver.kirkensnodhjelp.no | Symbolic gifts |
| Testamentarisk gave | kirkensnodhjelp.no/gi-stotte/arv | Bequest |
| Tax deduction | 500–25 000 NOK qualifies for skattefradrag | Norwegian rules |
| Jobs | kirkensnodhjelp.no/om-oss/ledige-stillinger | HQ Oslo + country-office openings |

There is no "bli medlem i Kirkens Nødhjelp" pathway. The supreme body (Representantskapet) is populated by delegates from Den norske kirkes bispedømmeråd, Kirkemøtet, Norges KFUK-KFUM, and six frikirker — individual Norwegians don't vote on NCA governance.

## Governance

- **Form**: Association (forening/lag/innretning) under Brreg, but structurally governed by vedtekter as a **fellesorgan for kirkesamfunn og menigheter**.
- **Generalsekretær**: Anne-Cecilie Kaltenborn (from Jan 2025, previously CEO of NHO Handel og Service). Succeeded Dagfinn Høybråten (2018–2024, previously Nordic Council Secretary General and government minister KrF).
- **Styreleder**: Tone Lindheim.
- **Styre (board)**: 9 members — elected by Representantskapet, plus Changemaker's elected leader, plus 2 NCA employee representatives.
- **Representantskapet (supreme organ)**: Delegates from each bispedømmeråd in Den norske kirke + 7 members of Kirkemøtet (inc. Samisk kirkeråd rep + youth <30) + representatives from Norges KFUK-KFUM + 2 delegates each from 6 frikirker (Den Evangelisk-Lutherske Frikirke, Det Norske Baptistsamfunn, Misjonskirken Norge, Frelsesarmeen, Metodistkirken Norge, Pinsebevegelsen) + NORME as observer.

### Governance documents (publicly available)

- Etiske retningslinjer + antikorrupsjonspolitikk
- Varslingsrutiner
- **Core Humanitarian Standard (CHS)** certification
- ACT Alliance Code of Conduct
- Årsrapport + Resultatrapport + Årsregnskap (published via Innsamlingskontrollen each April–May)

## Caveats and gaps

- **Formålsprosent discrepancy.** `ngo-landscape.md` cites 88.9%; Innsamlingskontrollen's 2024 one-pager shows **88.5%**; NCA's own marketing pages say **">90%"**. The 90% figure includes direct purpose costs net of certain cost-allocations; Innsamlingskontrollen uses the stricter gross ratio. Use 88.5% as the verifiable number and footnote NCA's 90% self-statement.
- **Country-list authority gap.** NCA's public "her jobber vi" page returned 404 during verification. The **30+ countries** claim is consistent across kirkensnodhjelp.no, Wikipedia, SNL but the authoritative current roster needs the 2024 årsberetning PDF (unparsed this pass). Flag this on the Compare-NGOs surface until manually verified.
- **Employee count ambiguity.** Brreg `antallAnsatte` = 165 (Oslo HQ point-in-time). NCA org page mentions **146 at HQ + ~850 globally**. `ngo-landscape.md` says **~450** — likely a mid-range figure from an earlier year. The 850 figure includes country-office national staff; the 165 figure is the Norwegian employer. All three are defensible depending on framing.
- **2024 income decline.** NCA's 2024 income (1 379 m) is **down 11% from 2023** (1 548 m) and down 11% from 2022 (1 545 m). This is a real contraction — worth verifying whether it's an accounting-period artifact or a genuine funding environment shift (Norad rammeavtale re-phasing, or UD humanitarian allocation timing). The Innsamlingskontrollen one-pager alone doesn't disambiguate. Would benefit from pulling the årsberetning narrative.
- **Changemaker boundary.** Changemaker is NCA's youth org but has its own org.nr and separate membership. For the chapter-finder surface we've treated it as distinct — that's correct for legal purposes but the programmatic integration is tight.
- **Fasteaksjonen year-over-year.** Confirmed 2025 total = NOK 36 m. Prior-year totals (2024 = ~30 m; 2023 = ~33 m; 2022 = ~33 m) are asserted in secondary press coverage but not fetched authoritatively here. The 50-year cumulative NOK 1.75 bn figure is from fasteaksjonen.no directly.
- **Norad rammeavtale phase.** The 2020 agreement was 5-year with ~350 m/year. As of April 2026 it's either in its renewed phase (2025–2029 window) or a bridging extension. The exact current-phase agreement terms aren't on the public Norad tildelinger page in machine-readable form.

## Surprising finding — the menighet-routed giving structure

The most analytically interesting property of NCA relative to the rest of the Tier C set:

**NCA has ~40 000 annual volunteer-equivalents and ~1 200 "collection points" across Norway — matching Røde Kors on raw counts — but zero of them are NCA entities.** The infrastructure is fully borrowed from Den norske kirke's konfirmasjonspipeline. NCA supplies campaign theme, curriculum, digital tooling, and bøsser; DNK supplies people, facilities, authority, and the annual calendar hook.

This has three consequences that matter for the app:

1. **The chapter-model framework does not fit, but the chapter-model *rendering* could.** If the Compare-NGOs surface shows "lokal tilstedeværelse", we can honestly render NCA as present in every kommune where DNK has a menighet (>95% of kommuner) — but labelled as routed-via-DNK, not owned-by-NCA.
2. **Volunteer-pathway UX needs a non-NRC-style "donasjonsbasert" slot.** The NRC template says "no volunteer pathway"; for NCA that's misleading. The right UX is "volunteer pathway exists but is institutionally mediated — action is to visit your local menighet during Lent" with a deep-link to `kirken.no/menighet`.
3. **Fundraising transparency is harder than for NRC.** NCA's 241.3 m in "innsamlede midler" aggregates pre-aggregated menighetsmidler with direct NCA donor flows. The individual-donor economic unit isn't identifiable in NCA's books — it passed through DNK's books first. This is defensible practice but means NCA cannot answer "how many unique givere do you have?" the way NRC can.

If we end up building a **Tier C-hybrid** template variant for NCA (and possibly Frelsesarmeen, which has a similar but inverted profile — own korps network + external ACT affiliations), the flagship differentiator would be this "institutionally-mediated mass engagement" concept.
