# Regnskogfondet / Rainforest Foundation Norway — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Regnskogfondet / Rainforest Foundation Norway (RFN)**, org.nr `985828806`. RFN is Norway's specialist rainforest-and-indigenous-rights NGO and the single most important civil-society partner to the Norwegian government's International Climate and Forest Initiative (NICFI / Klima- og skoginitiativet / KoS).

In our framework RFN is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): professional operator, no domestic chapter network, no member democracy, no volunteer pathway, donate-first engagement. What makes RFN structurally distinct from the other Tier C orgs — and worth flagging explicitly in the app — is that it functions as a **quasi-governmental funding channel**: a Norwegian state climate-and-forest priority, operating at roughly NOK 3–4 bn/year under NICFI, is partly delivered through RFN as an implementing partner. The charity sits inside a policy machine that the Norwegian state built and funds.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, regnskog.no, regjeringen.no and Wikipedia. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do — rainforest protection as an indigenous-partnership model

RFN's operating theory is that **rainforest is best protected by the people who live in it**. Rather than running its own field projects, RFN channels Norwegian state and philanthropic money to ~50–60 local and national partner organisations led by Indigenous peoples, forest communities, and national environmental/human-rights groups. This makes RFN unusual in the Norwegian bistand landscape: it is primarily a **grant-making and advocacy organisation**, not a direct-implementing operator like NRC or Kirkens Nødhjelp.

### Programme pillars

| Pillar | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Urfolks- og lokalsamfunnsrettigheter** (Indigenous and community rights) | Legal recognition of territories, tenure-rights campaigning, direct finance to IPLC (Indigenous Peoples and Local Communities) organisations |
| **Politisk påvirkning** (Policy and advocacy) | Norwegian, EU and global-forum advocacy on deforestation drivers: palm oil, soy, beef/leather, pulp/paper, tropical timber, minerals, biofuels |
| **Sustainable finance** | Campaigns targeting investors and commodity buyers; mobilising institutional capital behind deforestation-free supply chains (2024: 31 investors / USD 2.7 tn demanding deforestation-free nickel) |
| **Bevaring og skogforvaltning** (Conservation and forest management) | Technical support to partners establishing protected areas, indigenous reserves, monitoring systems |
| **Klimafinansiering** (Climate finance) | Policy work on NICFI, REDD+, and the new **Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF)** announced at COP30 Belém (Nov 2025) with a Norwegian pledge of up to USD 3 bn |

Source: regnskog.no/en/what-we-do, 2024 annual report.

### Flagship publications

- **State of the Tropical Rainforest** (2020) — billed as the world's first complete overview of the state of the tropical rainforest. The closest RFN has to a signature recurring flagship (though not yet annual).
- **State of Funding for Tenure Rights** (2nd edition, 2025) — tracks international donor funding for Indigenous peoples, local communities and Afro-descendant groups in tropical countries 2011–2024. Bistandsaktørenes barometer-equivalent for tenure finance.
- **Rainforest Payback** (original + *Rainforest Payback 2*, 2025) — quantifies how deforestation-induced climate changes hurt agricultural output, insurance costs and public subsidies in the Brazilian Amazon.
- **Falling Short** (2021) — the canonical report on the gap between promised and delivered direct finance to IPLCs.
- **Driving Deforestation** — European automotive supply chains and Brazilian leather.
- **Biofuel to the Fire** (2020) — palm oil and soy biofuel policy.

These reports are RFN's primary soft-power asset: they shape Norwegian parliamentary debates (Stortingets klima- og miljøkomité), KLD/UD positions, EUDR negotiations and international climate-finance fora.

### 2024 headline results (annual report)

- Improved protection for **172 000 km²** of rainforest through indigenous-rights and forest-management work.
- Establishment of **Mamberamo Foja National Park** (17 000 km²) in Indonesia.
- Creation of **Sierra del Divisor Occidental Indigenous Reserve** (5 150 km²) in Peru.
- Mobilised 31 investors (USD 2.7 trillion in assets) to demand deforestation-free nickel sourcing.
- Direct finance to IPLC partners up **36% over four years**.

## Where they work

RFN operates in **6 core rainforest countries** across 3 regions. Unlike NRC's 40-country footprint, the geography is tight and deliberate — each country is picked for its rainforest extent, indigenous population, and policy leverage.

| Region | Core countries | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Amazon / South America** | **Brazil**, **Peru**, **Colombia** | The Brazil programme is partly funded via a direct UD agreement with the Norwegian embassy in Brasília |
| **Central Africa / Congo Basin** | **Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC)** | RFN has a country office; partner network also extends regionally |
| **Southeast Asia / Oceania** | **Indonesia**, **Papua New Guinea** | Indonesia is historically the flagship Southeast Asia programme |

Ecuador, Malaysia and other rainforest nations show up in partner-network reach and campaign work but are not full country programmes.

Source: regnskog.no annual report 2024; regnskog.no/en/what-we-do/about-us.

## Money in — the quasi-governmental funding structure

This is the data point that differentiates RFN from every other Tier C org and deserves to be flagged explicitly on any app surface comparing funding transparency: **RFN is essentially a civil-society delivery arm for a Norwegian state climate priority**. The money is Norwegian taxpayer money, the policy is a Norwegian cabinet priority, and the implementing vehicle is a Norwegian NGO.

### Funding backbone (RFN's own framing)

RFN's finansiering page describes three Norwegian state sources as **"ryggraden" (the backbone)** of its operations, each a multi-year framework agreement:

1. **Norad rammeavtale** — Norwegian Agency for Development Cooperation framework agreement, the long-running core grant.
2. **Klima- og skoginitiativet (KoS-avtalen / NICFI)** — Norway's International Climate and Forest Initiative, administered jointly by **Klima- og miljødepartementet (KLD)** and Norad. This is the single largest Norwegian climate-foreign-policy instrument and the reason RFN is structurally positioned where it is.
3. **Utenriksdepartementet (UD)** — via a separate agreement with the **Norwegian Embassy in Brasília** funding the Brazil programme.

International philanthropic foundations and individual donors supplement the state backbone.

Exact donor-by-donor NOK figures from the 2024 annual accounts couldn't be extracted in this verification pass (the PDF is binary-encoded — see Caveats). Figures below are triangulated from Innsamlingskontrollen totals, the annual-report summary and RFN's public finansiering narrative.

### The NICFI / Klima- og skoginitiativet context

RFN's fortunes are tied to Norway's climate-and-forest spend. Key numbers for the app's Funding-transparency surface:

- **Total Norwegian rainforest spend 2024**: **NOK 4 billion / yr** (~USD 380m), after a 30% increase in 2024.
- **Extension to 2035**: Announced by PM Jonas Gahr Støre at UN General Assembly, September 2024 — NICFI extended five years beyond the previous 2030 horizon.
- **Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF)**: Announced by Norway at **COP30 Belém, 6 November 2025**. Norway pledged **up to USD 3 bn (~NOK 30 bn) over 10 years** in loans, capped at 20% of total fund, minimum USD 10 bn to be mobilised by end-2026, repayment by 2075. Led by **KLD with the PM's office**. Described by the government as "the largest single investment under NICFI since 2008."
- **RFN's own share** of the ~NOK 4 bn annual NICFI envelope: RFN's total 2024 income was NOK 328 m — so if we assume roughly half of that flows from the NICFI/KoS agreement (the single largest line), RFN is receiving perhaps **3–5%** of the annual NICFI envelope. The rest goes to multilateral funds (GCF, FCPF), bilateral agreements with Brazil/Indonesia/Colombia/Gabon, and other implementers. This matters for the framing: **RFN is a major NICFI channel, but NICFI is much bigger than RFN**.

### What this tells us (app implications)

- **Quasi-governmental framing is accurate.** State money for a state priority, delivered via an NGO. Unlike NRC (where US BHA, ECHO and UN pass-throughs diversify the donor mix) or Leger Uten Grenser Norge (100% private by policy), RFN sits near the opposite pole: dominant Norwegian-state funding for a specific Norwegian foreign-policy agenda.
- **Ambition to diversify.** RFN's own page states an aim that by 2030, international (philanthropic) funding should significantly exceed Norwegian public funding. That is a hedge against political risk: a change of government in Oslo (a Frp/Høyre coalition reducing NICFI, for instance) would be existential without diversification.
- **Lottstift is not the place to look.** Like the other institutionally-funded NGOs (NRC, Kirkens Nødhjelp, Plan, Redd Barna, Folkehjelpen), RFN's state money flows through framework agreements with Norad/UD/KLD, not through `tilskudd.lottstift.no` competitive ordninger. A naive Lottstift query for 985828806 will return little. The real money is in Statsbudsjettet kap. 1482 (Klima- og skogsatsing) and Norad rammeavtaler.
- **Policy-risk transparency.** For Jonas (transparency-focused donor) and Ola (data-curious observer), the RFN card should carry a clear "dominant Norwegian-state funding; policy-coupled risk" label that's absent from most other Tier C cards.

## Money out — programme and geographic split

RFN publishes for 2024:
- Total direct purpose costs: **NOK 290.9 m** (88.7% of income)
- Fundraising costs: **NOK 11.6 m** (3.5%)
- Administration costs: **NOK 22.2 m** (6.8%)

A programme split by region (Amazon vs Congo Basin vs Southeast Asia) is reported in the annual report narrative but not in the public Innsamlingskontrollen page as line items. The share-to-cause figure of ~88.4% is lower than NRC's >90% — consistent with the grant-making-plus-advocacy model (more policy and administrative overhead proportionally) rather than a direct-service humanitarian operation.

For app surfaces that need country-level expenditure, the authoritative sources are RFN's own annual report PDF and (for cross-partner tenure-finance flows) RFN's own *State of Funding for Tenure Rights* dataset.

## How to engage

RFN is **donor-only** for the Norwegian public. There is no member (beyond the 5 organisational members in the governance structure), no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no school-ambassador programme on the public site.

Verified pathways (regnskog.no/no/stott-oss):

| Pathway | URL / route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Regnskogsvokter** (monthly giver) | `vokter.regnskog.no/vipps/avtale/app/regnskogvokter/{200,300,500}` | The flagship ask. Branded as "Rainforest Guardian" — closest RFN has to a fadder product, but it is a monthly giver agreement, not a 1-to-1 sponsorship of a village/tribe/hectare |
| One-off donation (Vipps) | Vipps 3333 | Primary one-off route |
| One-off donation (bank) | Konto 1602 58 32500 | Traditional giro |
| Spleis (crowdfunding) | `spleis.no/org/4818` | Birthday/event campaigns |
| Testamentarisk gave (bequest) | `regnskog.no/no/testamentariske-gaver` | Funeral-/estate-context giving |
| Postkodelotteriet | `regnskog.no/no/stott-uss/postkodelotteriet-stotter-regnskogfondet` | Automatic monthly support via lottery membership — joined March 2026 |
| Nettbutikk (shop) | `vokter.regnskog.no/nettbutikk` | Merch / symbolic gifts |
| Bedrift (corporate) | `regnskog.no/no/bedrift` | Corporate partnerships (Fortum, Rema 1000 named) |
| Tax deduction | 500–25 000 NOK qualifies for skattefradrag | Norwegian rules |

Note on the "fadder" question: the brief asks about a Regnskogsfadder / sponsor-a-tribe or hectare model. RFN does **not** run a classic per-beneficiary fadder product like Plan or SOS-barnebyer. The closest equivalent is **Regnskogsvokter** — monthly subscription framed as "guarding the rainforest," without any 1-to-1 assignment to a village, tribe, or geographic unit.

Why no volunteer pathway? Same three structural reasons as NRC:
1. **Operational model** — partner-driven advocacy and grant-making, not direct delivery. No tasks for untrained Norwegian volunteers.
2. **Legal form** — forening with 5 organisational members; no individual-member structure.
3. **Where the volunteer ask sits** — the 5 member organisations (Naturvernforbundet, Natur og Ungdom etc.) have their own volunteer pathways and absorb demand from Norwegians wanting hands-on rainforest-adjacent activism.

For the app, the RFN chapter card in Compare-NGOs view should say "donasjonsbasert organisasjon — ingen lokallag, ingen frivilligplassering; frivillig engasjement kanaliseres gjennom medlemsorganisasjonene (Naturvernforbundet / Natur og Ungdom / Framtiden i våre hender / Utviklingsfondet / Miljøagentene)."

## Governance

- **Form**: Forening (FLI) with 5 institutional member organisations. Not a stiftelse despite the "fondet" in the name.
- **Generalsekretær**: Tørris Jæger, since April 2021. Previously generalsekretær in Norges Røde Kors. Replaced Øyvind Eggen (2018–2020), who left after internal turmoil; interim period 2020–2021 handled by deputy leadership.
- **Styreleder**: Marius Holm (September 2023–), ex-Zero CEO. Took over from Kari Bucher, who remained as nestleder.
- **Member organisations** (the governing "eiere" of the forening):
  - Naturvernforbundet (Friends of the Earth Norway)
  - Utviklingsfondet (Development Fund)
  - Framtiden i våre hender (Future in Our Hands)
  - Natur og Ungdom (Nature and Youth)
  - Miljøagentene (Eco-Agents, children's environmental organisation)
- **Board composition**: Elected by the 5 member organisations at årsmøte; mix of civil-society, policy and climate-finance backgrounds.

### Governance documents and compliance

- Approved by Innsamlingskontrollen.
- Annual accounts filed via Brreg and Innsamlingskontrollen; audited by BDO (per 2024 filing signatory).
- Norad-compliant anti-corruption and results-reporting regime (standard for Norad rammeavtalepartnere).

## Caveats and gaps

- **Annual accounts PDF parsing failed.** The `arsregnskap-regnskogfondet-2024.pdf` hosted on Innsamlingskontrollen is binary-encoded and couldn't be extracted as text in this verification pass. Exact donor-by-donor NOK amounts (Norad vs KoS/NICFI vs UD vs KLD vs international foundations) are in the Note on Tilskudd but were not read. All donor-share percentages above are triangulated/estimated; they should be tightened once the PDF is OCR'd. Recommended offline pass: `pdftotext arsregnskap-regnskogfondet-2024.pdf`.
- **Generalsekretær name.** The brief referenced "Tørres Rasmussen Eiken (interim?)". Verified from multiple sources (Naturpress, Panorama Nyheter, RFN's own site, Wikipedia): the correct name is **Tørris Jæger**, in post since April 2021 (not interim). The brief's version appears to be a misattribution — possibly conflated with a different person or an earlier interim arrangement in 2020–2021.
- **Share-to-cause comparison.** RFN's ~88.4% (2024) is materially lower than NRC's >90% and Kirkens Nødhjelp's ~90%. This is not a red flag — it reflects RFN's higher proportional admin/policy overhead as a grant-making-plus-advocacy shop rather than a direct-service operator. Worth explaining rather than hiding in the UI.
- **NICFI share estimate is rough.** The "~3–5% of annual NICFI envelope" figure is my triangulation (NICFI NOK 4 bn / RFN ~NOK 150–200 m NICFI-ish). Should be replaced with the exact 2024 KoS-avtale figure once the annual accounts PDF is parsed. Regjeringen's NICFI annual accounting would also be a cross-check.
- **Employee count.** Brreg reports 69; RFN's own about-us page doesn't publish a headcount. 69 is point-in-time Brreg — may understate if contractors and seconded partner staff are significant.
- **Member-organisation composition.** The 5 member organisations are sometimes listed with ForUM (Forum for utvikling og miljø) as a sixth historical co-founder; current governing membership is the 5 listed above per RFN's own statements.
- **Postkodelotteriet joining.** Picked up as "March 2026" in one RFN description — so the Postkodelotteriet revenue stream will start appearing in 2026 accounts, not 2024/2025. Material for comparing RFN's donor mix over time.
- **Surprising findings worth surfacing**:
  1. **The TFFF USD 3 bn pledge** is huge — at ~NOK 30 bn it dwarfs RFN's entire institutional history of funding. RFN's lobby work around TFFF is probably the highest-leverage activity in its 2025–2035 window, but the TFFF money goes into a multilateral fund, not directly to RFN.
  2. **RFN's own diversification aim** — its stated target that by 2030 international (philanthropic) funding should exceed Norwegian public funding — is a direct admission that the quasi-governmental funding structure is a concentrated political risk RFN is actively trying to reduce.
  3. **Marius Holm as styreleder** is noteworthy: the ex-CEO of Zero (Norway's most influential climate policy think-tank) now chairs the largest Norwegian civil-society recipient of the state climate-and-forest envelope. That overlap is a governance feature worth noting in any deeper transparency panel.
