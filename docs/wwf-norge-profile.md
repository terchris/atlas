# WWF Verdens naturfond — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Stiftelsen WWF Verdens naturfond** (WWF Norge / WWF Norway), org.nr `952330071`. WWF Norge is the Norwegian node of the global WWF network — a nature-conservation stiftelse that runs both a domestic Norwegian advocacy operation (rainforest, predators, ocean, Oslofjord, natural-area loss) and an international programme portfolio delivered in partnership with WWF-International and WWF country offices.

In our framework WWF Norge is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a professional stiftelse with no domestic chapter network, no member democracy, and no volunteer pathway in the classical chapter sense. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces (Red Cross, Sanitetskvinnene, Nasjonalforeningen, Folkehjelp) doesn't apply, so WWF Norge doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It does still appear in four places in the app — Compare NGOs, Funding transparency, Global-context panel during nature/climate crises, and (distinctively) **Campaign action (Ta et standpunkt)** — and this profile is the data source behind those surfaces.

WWF Norge is one of three Tier C orgs in our landscape that expose a **fourth engagement pathway: public petition / campaign action**, alongside Amnesty International Norge and Naturvernforbundet. This is the defining UX implication of the profile.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, wwf.no, tilskudd.lottstift.no, Wikipedia (NO) and wwf.panda.org. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do

WWF Norge's work is organised around **six core programme areas**. The taxonomy is stable across wwf.no's site architecture (slik-arbeider-wwf) and maps onto WWF International's Practice framework.

| Programme (no) | Programme (en) | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Klima og energi** | Climate & energy | Norwegian and international climate policy, energy transition, fossil phase-out advocacy, nature-based climate solutions |
| **Hav og fiskeri** | Oceans | Sustainable fisheries, marine protected areas, Oslofjorden, coastal fisheries reform, **anti-deep-sea-mining** (flagship Norwegian campaign), salmon aquaculture reform |
| **Ferskvann** | Freshwater | Rivers, watercourse restoration, vassdragsvern |
| **Skog** | Forest | Norwegian old-growth forest (skog), global rainforest (regnskog) protection, FSC-certification advocacy |
| **Arktis og polhav** | Arctic | Polhav, isbjørn, svalbardsel, climate–biodiversity intersection |
| **Arter og villmark** | Species & wildlife | Predator protection in Norway (ulv, bjørn, gaupe, jerv), global tiger/panda/elephant conservation via WWF network, CITES advocacy |

Source: wwf.no/slik-arbeider-wwf (live, 2026-04-18).

Cross-cutting: **plastforurensing** (plastic pollution — the 2020 TV-aksjonen theme, still an active workstream with the global plastic treaty negotiations running through 2025–2026); **biomangfold** (biodiversity as the umbrella concept); **bærekraftige matsystemer**.

### Flagship initiatives

- **Pandaklubben** — WWF's klubb for små naturvenner (children 0–17). Quasi-membership for children with magazine, cards and activities; fourth structural engagement pathway alongside fadder, monthly giver, and petition. Directly modeled on (and shares branding with) Swedish WWF's Pandaklubben. URL: `wwf.no/engasjer-deg/pandaklubben`.
- **Earth Hour Norway** — global WWF hour-without-lights campaign on the last Saturday of March (29 March 2025, 28 March 2026 — Earth Hour's 20th anniversary). WWF Norge runs the Norwegian surface: municipal sign-ups, monument blackouts (Slottet, Stortinget, town halls), school and corporate participation. URL: `wwf.no/engasjer-deg/earth-hour`.
- **TV-aksjonen 2020** — WWF Norge held the 2020 TV-aksjonen (theme: plastic pollution in oceans), raising **NOK 239 m** for programmes in Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines and Thailand. This funding cycle runs through 2025–2026 and underwrites a significant share of WWF Norge's international plastic programming.
- **Havpanelet engagement** — WWF Norge is one of the most visible civil-society pressure groups around the **High Level Panel for a Sustainable Ocean Economy** (Havpanelet), which Norway's PM co-chairs with Palau. In 2024 WWF (with Greenpeace and others) publicly demanded PM Jonas Gahr Støre step down as Havpanelet co-chair unless Norway reversed deep-sea mining plans. This is a distinctive advocacy-lobbying posture — not typical of Tier C operators.
- **Sjømatguiden** (Seafood guide) — consumer-facing tool ranking fish/shellfish species on a green/yellow/red sustainability scale; distinctive WWF-branded public-interest product with direct consumer-behaviour ambition.
- **Rapporter og notater** — WWF Norge publishes policy reports and notater (`wwf.no/slik-arbeider-wwf/rapporter-og-notater`) used as inputs to Storting høringer and UD consultations. Functions as a think-tank arm alongside the campaigning arm.

## Where they work

WWF Norge is structurally **dual**: a Norwegian domestic advocacy organisation *and* a funder/implementer within the global WWF network.

### Domestic (Norway)

No chapters. Oslo HQ only. Work is delivered through:
- Policy advocacy (Storting and regjering — påvirkning og partnerskap avdeling)
- Public campaigning (petition pipeline — see `/engasjer-deg`)
- Research and reporting (rapporter og notater)
- Corporate partnerships (næringslivspartnere)
- School-facing content (Fugleskole, Havskole)
- Youth/children's engagement (Pandaklubben)

### International

WWF Norge does **not** run its own country offices. International programme funding (notably the TV-aksjonen 2020 plastic portfolio: Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines, Thailand; plus regnskog programmes in the Amazon, Congo Basin and Borneo) is delivered **through WWF-International and WWF country offices** as transfer partners. This is the structural model the brief asked us to clarify: WWF Norge raises money in Norway, spends roughly half on Norwegian operations and advocacy, and channels the remainder to WWF-International / partner offices that operate on the ground.

Exact split not published on wwf.no's public pages; needs extraction from `arsberetning-stiftelsen-wwf-verdens-naturfond-2024.pdf` note 2. See Caveats.

WWF Norge does not publish country-level expenditure or beneficiary figures on its public site. For global context, WWF-International's annual report (wwf.panda.org) consolidates country-office activity but does not break down by donor-network-member funder.

### Historical share (2020 reference, Wikipedia)

- Offentlige midler (Norad + UD): **~55%** of 2020 income
- Private (members + corporate): **~45%**

This split has likely shifted **more private** since 2020 as Postkodelotteriet has scaled and TV-aksjonen-2020 cash has flowed. The 2024 split is not publicly disclosed in percentage form — needs extraction from the 2024 årsberetning PDF note 2.

### What this tells us

- **Private-heavy relative to the Tier C humanitarian cluster.** NRC and CARE Norge are 70–90% institutional. WWF Norge is likely closer to **50–50 private / institutional** in 2024. Fadder is the flagship pathway and the dominant recurring-giver framing.
- **Postkodelotteriet is a first-tier funder.** As a founding Norwegian Postkodelotteriet beneficiary (2018), WWF Norge's annual lottery allocation is material. Exact NOK not published on lottery org page but is in 2024 årsberetning note.
- **Norad + UD is still significant but minority.** Norway's rainforest-protection funding (Klima- og skoginitiativet) is channelled primarily through Regnskogfondet (NOK ~328m 2024), not WWF Norge — so WWF Norge's Norad share is smaller than a naive reading of "rainforest NGO" would suggest.
- **2024 was a deficit year.** Income 265.0m vs total costs (301.3 + 21.8 + 9.9 = 333.1m) = ~**NOK –68m operating deficit**. Very large relative to income. Likely explained by multi-year TV-aksjonen-2020 programme cash disbursing in 2024 from reserves built in earlier years (timing mismatch, not structural). Worth flagging in transparency UX; verify pattern in 2025 once accounts land.
- **No Norwegian state funding via Lottstift ordninger** (same pattern as NRC, CARE, Kirkens Nødhjelp). The Lottstift 8.25m is purely momskompensasjon — a technical VAT refund, not programme funding. Statsbudsjettet kap. 141/161/162 (klima, miljø, bistand) is where the real UD/Norad/KLD money lives.

### Why no volunteer pathway

1. **Stiftelse form** — no members; governance is styret only.
2. **No chapter network** — unlike Naturvernforbundet (which is a forening with ~50 fylkes/lokallag), WWF Norge has never built a domestic chapter structure. All programme delivery is through Oslo HQ staff and external partners.
3. **Advocacy + programme model** — WWF's international programme work is delivered by paid WWF country-office staff, not Norwegian volunteers.

For the app, WWF Norge's chapter card in Compare-NGOs should show "**donasjonsbasert + kampanjeaktivisme — ingen lokallag, ingen frivilligplassering, men aktiv underskriftskampanje-portefølje**" rather than an empty volunteer slot.

## Part of WWF International — what the network relationship means

WWF Norge is a **national office** within the WWF network (~100 national offices + Secretariat in Gland, Switzerland). Network relationships are governed by WWF International's network standards; each national office is a legally independent entity in its home country but operates under a shared brand, mission framework (Practices: Climate & Energy, Food, Forests, Freshwater, Oceans, Wildlife), and programme coordination.

Financial flow:
- **Norway-raised income stays partly in Norway** (staff, Norwegian advocacy, domestic campaigns, fundraising) and **partly transfers out** (to WWF-International and/or specific country offices) to co-fund global programmes WWF Norge is a lead or co-lead partner in (e.g. TV-aksjonen 2020 plastic programme → WWF-Indonesia, WWF-Philippines, WWF-Vietnam, WWF-Thailand).
- **Exact transfer %** not published on wwf.no's public pages. The 2024 årsberetning PDF note 2 contains the breakdown ("bidrag til WWF-nettverket" / "overføringer til partnerkontor") but the PDF is not WebFetch-parseable. Best practical estimate: 40–55% of programme spend transfers out of Norway, given the international programme portfolio's scale.
- WWF Norge does **not** receive programme funding from WWF-International's central pool; the flow is Norway-out, not in. The exception is shared services (WWF Network Standards, global brand, Practices coordination) which are paid for via a network levy on national offices.

This is a distinctly different network economy from e.g. UNICEF-komiteen Norge or SOS-barnebyer (which primarily **fundraise in Norway and channel abroad**) — WWF Norge is more similar to Plan International Norge and Redd Barna: a legally independent national office doing both domestic advocacy *and* international programme funding.

## Thematic overlap — WWF Norge vs Naturvernforbundet vs Regnskogfondet vs Sabima

WWF Norge is one of four Norwegian NGOs in the nature/climate thematic cluster. The four occupy **distinct but adjacent** structural slots that Compare-NGOs should make legible:

| Org | Tier | Form | Geography | Engagement | Distinctive positioning |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WWF Verdens naturfond** | **C** | Stiftelse | Global + Norway | Donate + petition + Pandaklubben + corporate | Global WWF network membership; TV-aksjonen 2020 residual; highest petition volume |
| **Naturvernforbundet** | A | Forening | Primarily Norway | Member + petition + volunteer + donate | Fylkeslag + lokallag; democratic member org; founded 1914 |
| **Regnskogfondet** | C | Stiftelse | International (rainforest countries) | Donate | Norad/Klimainvest-dominated; rainforest-specialist; no petitions |
| **Sabima** | Umbrella | Forening av foreninger | Norway | Member orgs only | 13 medlemsorganisasjoner (Norsk Ornitologisk Forening, Norsk Botanisk Forening, osv); not a direct-engagement org |

For app implementation:
- **Compare-NGOs** should surface all four when user signal is "nature/climate/biodiversity" — with clear labels distinguishing structural forms (Tier C stiftelse vs Tier A forening vs umbrella).
- **Thematic clustering** is orthogonal to structural clustering — same pattern as N.K.S. / CARE / Plan for women-and-girls.
- **Petition pathway surface** groups WWF Norge with Naturvernforbundet (and Amnesty Norge in the human-rights cluster), not with other Tier C operators like NRC or Leger Uten Grenser.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Lottstift figure discrepancy.** Brief baseline cites 11.4m Lottstift (~4% of income). tilskudd.lottstift.no currently shows only **8.253m momskompensasjon 2024** with a disclaimer "alle tildelinger for 2024 er ikke publisert". Gap likely closes when additional 2024 grant rounds publish (possibly additional momskomp tranches, Ekstraordinær momskomp, or specific ordninger). Baseline figure is plausible but not yet fully reconciled with Lottstift's published data.
- **Formålsprosent reconciliation.** Baseline says 90.3%. Innsamlingskontrollen's raw 2024 numbers yield **90.4%** by (formål / total costs). Close enough — rounding. The 2024 direct-purpose-cost ratio vs income is 113.7% (deficit year — see below) which should be distinguished carefully from the formål / totale kostnader ratio when displayed.
- **2024 operating deficit**. Income 265.0m vs total costs 333.1m = NOK –68m. Very large in absolute and relative terms (–25.7% of income). Almost certainly a timing mismatch: TV-aksjonen 2020 raised 239m in a single year and is being disbursed across 2020–2026 through WWF partner offices, so 2024 cost accounting includes prior-year raised funds. Requires Årsberetning 2024 narrative to confirm. Flag in transparency UX.
- **Income drop is not real.** 2023 → 2024 income went from 265.83m to 264.96m — effectively flat (–0.3%). The apparent jump from 2022 (209.36m) to 2023 reflects partial-year TV-aksjonen cash landing plus private-giving growth post-pandemic, not a one-off event in 2023.
- **Transfer-to-WWF-International share not public.** The structural question the brief asked — what share of Norway-raised income transfers out to WWF-International / country offices vs stays in Norway — is not disclosed on wwf.no's public pages. Number exists in Årsberetning 2024 PDF note 2 ("bidrag til nettverket") but PDF is binary-parse-fail via WebFetch. Worth running headless-browser extraction for the exact figure. Best estimate is 40–55% of programme spend transfers abroad, but this is inferred.
- **Staff count variance.** Brreg `antallAnsatte` is 82 (Oslo HQ). wwf.no/kontakt-og-ansatte lists ~90+ named employees. Wikipedia (2020 data) says ~70 FTE. All three are defensible for different definitions/dates. `ngo-landscape.md` baseline uses 82 — matches Brreg.
- **Founding-year ambiguity (minor).** 1970-12-14 is both the stiftelsesdato and the organisational-history date. Name changed 1989 (Verdens Villmarksfond → WWF Verdens naturfond). Registered in Enhetsregisteret 1995-03-12 (not a re-registration as stiftelse, just Enhetsregisteret rollout). App should display "Founded 1970" with a footnote about the 1989 renaming — no two-date issue like NRC or CARE.
- **Members vs supporters semantics**. WWF Norge has no formal members in a governance sense (stiftelse). It has støttemedlemmer, faddere and Pandaklubben-medlemmer who are donors/supporters. Adult "bli medlem" framing is used loosely on the site but doesn't carry governance implications. For the app, treat WWF as "no member pathway" in the same slot as other stiftelser; surface Pandaklubben as a dedicated children's pathway.
- **Board roster partial**. Styreleder (Erik Osmundsen) + 5 named external members + 1 ansattvalgt + deputy verified. Nestleder not identified on public page. Would need follow-up fetch to styredokumenter or Årsberetning for nestleder designation.
- **Petition URLs require percent-encoding**. Norwegian characters (å, ø) in several `/engasjer-deg` URLs need URL-encoding when deep-linking. Implementation detail for the campaign-action surface.
- **Annual report PDF parsing failed** (same limitation as NRC and CARE). Programme-level spend, country-level transfers, exact donor split exist in the PDF but require non-WebFetch extraction.
