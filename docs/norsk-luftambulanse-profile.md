# Norsk Luftambulanse / SNLA — organisation profile

Reference profile for **Stiftelsen Norsk Luftambulanse (SNLA)**, org.nr `939483136`. SNLA is — by member count — the largest member/supporter organisation in Norway: **approximately 706 000 støttemedlemmer and ~4 000 bedriftsmedlemmer**, generating NOK 410m in 2024 income. Crucially, the foundation does **not** operate the rescue helicopters the public associates with the brand. That work runs through a wholly-owned subsidiary under a state contract (see "The dual structure" below).

In our framework SNLA sits on the **Tier C / borderline C** line (`ngo-landscape.md` §5). It is a professional stiftelse with no member democracy and no volunteer operational pathway, but it diverges from pure Tier C (NRC, SOS-barnebyer, Leger Uten Grenser) in two ways: (a) it has a geographically distributed structure of **13 helicopter bases + 5 regional supporter councils (regionråd)** that a naïve reader could mistake for chapters; (b) the 706 000-strong støttemedlem base is orders of magnitude larger than any comparable Tier C. The app treats it as Tier C — donate-first, no chapter finder, no volunteer slot — but the Compare-NGOs surface should flag the støttemedlem number explicitly, and the Funding transparency surface should show a coverage map of the 13 bases.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, norskluftambulanse.no, luftambulanse.no and Wikipedia. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## The dual structure — critical to understand

SNLA is not one organisation; it is a group of three legal entities whose public brand deliberately merges. For the app's transparency surfaces, this split has to be made explicit — otherwise users attribute the NOK 410m donor income to rescue-helicopter operations it does not in fact fund.

| Entity | Role | Funding source | Employees | Org.nr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stiftelsen Norsk Luftambulanse** | Foundation. Research, training, førstehjelpskurs, public advocacy, fundraising, støttemedlem programme, owner of the subsidiaries | **Private** — 81% innsamlede midler (støttemedlem + bedrift + enkeltdonasjon + testament) | 204 | 939 483 136 |
| **Norsk Luftambulanse AS** (NLA AS / NLA Helikopter) | **Operator** of all 13 rescue-helicopter bases on behalf of Luftambulansetjenesten HF (the state's air-ambulance HF, owned by the four RHFs) | **State-contracted** — under a ~5 bn NOK / 10-year direct-award contract from 2028 (previous 2018 contract similar scale). Annual operator revenue ~2 bn NOK | ~400+ (pilots, paramedics, engineers) | 930 329 797 |
| **Norsk Luftambulanse Teknologi AS** | Owns helicopter-base real estate (Trondheim, Stavanger, Dombås + others); develops EMS technology | Mixed (rent from HF + foundation R&D grants) | smaller | separate |

Three consequences matter for any transparency surface:

1. **The helicopters are state healthcare, not charity.** When a rescue helicopter flies a patient in Norway, that mission is paid by the four Regional Health Authorities (RHFs) via Luftambulansetjenesten HF, routed to NLA AS under the operator contract. It is not paid by the 706 000 støttemedlemmer.
2. **The foundation's 410m is R&D + training + advocacy money.** It funds: ~30–40 PhD stipendiater in prehospital acute medicine, the development helicopter ("flygende laboratorium"), first-aid courses (førstehjelpskurs) for the public, equipment development (blind-flying tech, weather cameras, ice-protection), international exchange, advocacy campaigns (e.g. CT-in-helicopter, stroke ambulances), and the fundraising/member-service apparatus itself.
3. **Brand ambiguity is a feature, not a bug.** SNLA's communications habitually say "vi flyr 13 legehelikopterbaser" — technically true (the subsidiary does), but phrased in a way that implies the foundation's fundraising keeps the rescue service in the air. It doesn't; the state contract does. Støttemedlem money funds the **research and innovation layer around** the operator, which is material and valuable, but categorically different from the operational service itself.

The app should show, in the Funding transparency panel, a one-line disclosure along the lines of: *"Støttemedlemskap finansierer forskning, førstehjelpsopplæring og utviklingsarbeid. Selve redningshelikopterdriften finansieres av staten via Luftambulansetjenesten HF, med NLA AS som operatør."*

## What they do

The foundation organises around **four programme pillars**, all clearly distinct from operating the helicopters themselves:

| Pillar | Norwegian | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Research** | Forskning | 30+ PhD stipendiater, 6 research clusters across Norway, topics: prehospital stroke, heart attack, pain management, hypothermia, neonatal transport, trauma immunology |
| **Education & competence** | Utdanning og kompetanse | Akuttmedisinsk utdanning for ambulance personnel, simulator training, national/international exchange |
| **First aid for the public** | Førstehjelpsopplæring | Førstehjelpskurs (in-person + digital), "Hvem kan redde liv?"-kampanjer, GameStroke app |
| **Advocacy & public voice** | Talerør for pasienten | Equity-of-access campaigns, media presence on prehospital medicine, white papers (e.g. stroke ambulance, CT-in-helicopter) |

### Flagship initiatives

- **Forskningsstøtte** — funds prehospital acute-medicine research that the state does not fund directly. Flagship projects include the eSTROKE app (paramedic stroke triage), the PreMeFen trial (inhaled pain relief vs morphine), brain-ultrasound in flight, and the CT-in-helicopter programme.
- **Utviklingshelikopteret ("flying laboratory")** — a dedicated helicopter used for technology testing (weather cameras, ice protection, blind-flying avionics) before fleet-wide rollout.
- **Akuttdagen** — annual event on SNLA's property at Haug with public demonstrations, research showcases and støttemedlem engagement.
- **Akuttmagasinet** — membership magazine (five issues/year) distributed to støttemedlemmer; functions as both retention and advocacy channel.
- **Arctic Race beredskap** — seasonal first-aid coverage partnerships (visibility campaign more than operational commitment).

(The exact roster has shifted over time — Harstad/Narvik was replaced by Evenes; Finnmark base provision is split between fixed-wing and rotor depending on year. Live verification against luftambulanse.no recommended before production.)

For the app, this list underpins a **coverage-map** visualisation, not a chapter-finder. A user should be able to see "nearest rescue-helicopter base" but must not be invited to "join your local chapter" — no such concept exists in this organisation.

Denmark: NLA AS also operates 4 bases there under a separate Danish health-authority contract — out of scope for this profile but relevant context.

## Money in — funding composition

SNLA's income is **overwhelmingly Norwegian private** — the inverse of NRC's institutional profile. This is the contrast that matters when Compare-NGOs surfaces SNLA next to Flyktninghjelpen.

### 2024 income breakdown (Innsamlingskontrollen)

| Source | NOK (thousand) | Share |
|---:|---:|---:|
| Innsamlede midler (støttemedlem + bedrift + enkeltdonasjon + testament) | 332 634 | 81.1% |
| Annen inntekt (finansinntekter, salg, kurs) | 77 446 | 18.9% |
| Offentlige tilskudd | 0 | 0% |
| **Total** | **410 080** | **100%** |

### What this tells us

- **Zero Norwegian state grants to the foundation in 2024** (offentlige tilskudd = 0). State money reaches the group only via the NLA AS operator contract, not via the foundation.
- **Private giving is the engine.** 81% of income is directly donor-funded — the highest private share of any top-10 Norwegian NGO by income. No Norad rammeavtale, no UD frame, no EU/ECHO exposure.
- **Støttemedlem is the dominant channel.** Standard tier is 200 kr/år; many givers move to 50–100 kr/month (i.e. 600–1 200 kr/year). Bedriftsmedlemskap (~4 000 businesses) typically pays 2 500–25 000 kr/year depending on tier.
- **2022 had NOK 46.5m in public grants** — likely COVID-era or one-off; it has gone to zero by 2024. Worth flagging as a year-on-year delta.
- **Cost structure**: 71.5% direct-purpose cost ratio with 24.4% fundraising cost — the fundraising ratio is high by sector norms (NRC at 1.5%, Røde Kors central at ~8%). This reflects how expensive it is to maintain a 706 000-person recurring-giving programme with magazine, events and regional engagement.

### Funding flows outside Lottstift

Same pattern as other Tier C orgs: a Lottstift query for 939483136 will return little. The state money that reaches the group does so through:
- **Luftambulansetjenesten HF** (operator contract to NLA AS, not to the foundation)
- **Helse Sør-Øst** / RHF research grants (minor; to named projects)
- **Norges forskningsråd** (project-specific; modest)

None of these are "ordninger" in the Lottstift sense.

## Money out — where the 410m goes

Innsamlingskontrollen 2024 breakdown:
- **Direct purpose costs**: NOK 282.6m (68.9% of income, 71.5% of total costs)
- **Fundraising costs**: NOK 99.9m (24.4% of income)
- **Administration**: NOK 12.5m (3.0% of income)
- **Surplus**: NOK 15.2m (4% — moved to equity, used for research endowment)

Direct-purpose costs break down roughly as (foundation self-reporting):
- **Medical research**: ~250m NOK/year (budget figure the foundation cites)
- **First aid and public education**: smaller but meaningful share
- **Advocacy and publications (Akuttmagasinet, campaigns)**: moderate
- **Member service and regional council support**: treated partly as direct purpose, partly as fundraising

Note: the 275m NOK/year figure sometimes cited publicly for "air ambulance improvement" is the **contract allocation under the state direct-award (2028 onwards)** — not SNLA foundation spending.

## How to engage

SNLA's engagement portfolio looks diverse but functions primarily as a **donor-relationship ladder**. There is no operational volunteer pathway, no member democracy in the Sanitetskvinnene sense, and no chapter you can walk into.

| Pathway | Route | Primary / Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| **Støttemedlem** | `norskluftambulanse.no/stottemedlem/` — monthly AvtaleGiro (from ~50 kr/month standard, 100+ kr/month "ekstra") or annual 200–500 kr | Primary |
| **Bedriftsmedlem** | Several tiers, 2 500–25 000+ kr/year | Primary (B2B) |
| **Enkeltdonasjon** | Vipps, card, bank | Secondary |
| **Testamentarisk gave** | Legal guide on site | Long-tail major-gift channel |
| **Minnegave** | Memorial gift at funerals | Secondary |
| **Regionrådsmedlem** | Elected at regional meetings (April), 2-year term; 30–45 people nationwide | Governance-adjacent, not volunteer work |
| **Researcher / stipendiat** | Open calls through R&D department | Professional pathway |
| **Førstehjelpskurs (deltaker)** | Public sign-up for first-aid training | Engagement-as-learner |
| **SNLA-veteraner** | Alumni network for former employees / researchers | Niche, low-volume |
| **Tax deduction** | 500–25 000 kr qualifies | Standard Norwegian rules |

### On støttemedlem as "membership"

The 706 000 number is real and extraordinary — but it is a **donor relationship dressed in membership language**, not member democracy. Specifically:

- Støttemedlemmer do not vote on foundation business. The stiftelse is governed by its styre under stiftelsesloven; members have no formal role in general assemblies (there is no generalforsamling — a foundation has no such body).
- What støttemedlemmer get: *Akuttmagasinet* five times a year, invitations to Akuttdagen, and the right to stand for election to one of the 5 regional councils.
- The regional councils have **advisory input** into the board's selection committee but no binding governance authority.
- Comparing 706k SNLA støttemedlemmer to 135k Røde Kors medlemmer or 33k Sanitetskvinnene medlemmer is apples-to-oranges: the Røde Kors / Sanitetskvinnene members elect delegates who elect boards; SNLA members do not.

For the app, label SNLA's 706k figure explicitly as "støttemedlemmer (givertype)" rather than "medlemmer" to avoid false equivalence with chapter-anchored member democracies. The Compare-NGOs surface should have a tooltip that makes this distinction visible.

### Why no operational volunteer pathway?

- **Safety-critical operations.** Helicopter missions are flown by professional pilots, paramedics and anaesthesiologists on the NLA AS payroll — zero room for volunteer work.
- **Research work is credentialed.** The 30+ stipendiater are PhD researchers, not volunteers.
- **Stiftelse form.** No membership layer with rights.
- **Where volunteers would sit** — first-aid-course delivery, Akuttdagen stands, and regional-council work — is handled either by paid staff or by støttemedlemmer in a clearly advisory role.

For the app, this means SNLA's card in Compare-NGOs should show "donasjonsbasert stiftelse — 706 000 støttemedlemmer, ingen lokallag, ingen operasjonell frivilligplassering" rather than an empty volunteer slot.

## Governance

- **Form**: Stiftelse, regulated under stiftelsesloven; supervised by Stiftelsestilsynet. Foundation capital NOK 920 525.
- **Generalsekretær**: **Stephen J. M. Sollid**, MD, professor of prehospital acute medicine, from 1 April 2024. Previously klinikksjef for prehospital klinikk at OUS; former sjeflege at SNLA. Succeeded Hans Morten Lossius (2015–2024), who now holds a 50% R&D advisory role.
- **Styreleder**: **Unni Grete Farestveit** (per Proff / SNL / 2024 press release citing her selection of Sollid).
- **Styret**: Multi-member board, mix of medical, business and public-administration profiles. Full roster not extracted in this verification pass; requires a direct fetch to the foundation's styret page (was 404 at time of check).
- **Selection committee**: Three members per region × 5 regions = 15 people nationally; proposes board candidates.

### Governance artefacts

- Årsrapport (published annually; hosted via Issuu under `norskluftambulanse` channel)
- Årsregnskap (Innsamlingskontrollen)
- Stiftelsesvedtekter (supervised by Stiftelsestilsynet)
- Member magazine `Akuttmagasinet` functions as a quasi-transparency channel to the donor base

## Caveats and gaps

- **706 000 støttemedlemmer figure**: sourced from Wikipedia citing foundation materials; the foundation's own public pages more cautiously say "over 300 000 støttespillere". The 706k likely includes all active recurring donors + corporate members + historical registrations; the 300k likely counts only active monthly givers. Worth reconciling against the 2024 årsrapport before publishing externally. Treat 706k as "headline / largest-member-base" figure with a footnote; use 300k+ as the conservative floor.
- **Share-to-cause figure**: Innsamlingskontrollen calculates 71.5% for 2024; our briefing cited 69%. Both are defensible; the 69% figure may reflect a different cost-classification (e.g. treating some member-service costs as overhead). Label methodology.
- **NLA AS org.nr 930329797**: confirmed in web search results but not individually re-verified against Brreg in this pass. Verify before use in app.
- **Base list**: the canonical 13-base roster drifts (Finnmark base placement, Evenes vs Harstad/Narvik). Production UI should pull live from `luftambulanse.no` rather than hard-coding.
- **Board roster**: Styreleder confirmed (Unni Grete Farestveit), but full board list not extracted. The foundation's `/styret-og-ledelse/` URL returned 404 at verification time — try `/hvem-er-vi/` plus Proff as fallback.
- **Annual report PDF not parsed**: Issuu-hosted PDFs require separate extraction. Programme-by-programme cost split and detailed research-project allocation exist there.
- **Employees**: Brreg shows 204 for the foundation. The group total (including NLA AS pilots/medics/engineers) is substantially larger; 2009 figures cited 220 at NLA AS alone; current figure likely 400+. Verify against NLA AS årsrapport.
- **Founded year**: Brreg registration 1995-02-19, but organisation was established 1977-11-30. Display "Grunnlagt 1977" with footnote.
- **Regional councils counted as governance, not activity**: any UI that implies regionråd = lokallag is wrong. Regionråd exist to advise the board and field ~30–45 engaged støttemedlemmer; they are not public-facing drop-in structures.
