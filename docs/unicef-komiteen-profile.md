# UNICEF-komiteen Norge — organisation profile

Reference profile for **UNICEF-komiteen i Norge** (public brand: UNICEF Norge), org.nr `915972438`. UNICEF-komiteen Norge is the Norwegian national committee of the United Nations Children's Fund — a private fundraising and advocacy foundation that channels Norwegian private giving into UNICEF's global children's rights work, and simultaneously runs a domestic children's-rights programme inside Norway.

In our framework UNICEF-komiteen is a **Tier C** organisation (`ngo-landscape.md` §5): a professional operator-foundation with no domestic chapter network, no member democracy, and no volunteer pathway for the Norwegian public. The chapter-anchored model that drives Tier A surfaces (Red Cross, Sanitetskvinnene, Nasjonalforeningen, Folkehjelp, etc.) doesn't apply, so UNICEF-komiteen doesn't get an `activities.md` or `activity-indicator-matrix.md`. It does appear in two places in the app — **Compare NGOs** and **Funding transparency** — and this profile is the data source behind those surfaces. It is also the canonical example of a Norwegian NGO with a **deliberate no-Norwegian-state-money** funding stance, which is a first-order point in the transparency story.

Verified live on **2026-04-18** against Brreg, Innsamlingskontrollen, unicef.no, and no.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNICEF_Norge. Caveats and gaps at the bottom.

## What they do — the two missions

UNICEF-komiteen Norge is structurally unusual among Tier C operator-foundations: it runs **two distinct missions** under one roof, and the split is the single most important shaping fact for the profile. Most Norwegian operator-stiftelser (NRC, SOS, MSF Norge, CARE, Plan, Regnskogfondet) are international-only; UNICEF Norge is international-first but operates a substantial domestic programme as well.

### Mission 1 — International: funding UNICEF global + Norwegian-facing advocacy

The dominant mission, by both headcount and spend. UNICEF-komiteen does not run field operations itself. Instead it:

- **Raises private money in Norway** and forwards it to UNICEF International (New York HQ) as earmarked or unearmarked contributions.
- **Represents UNICEF to Norwegian audiences** through information work, media, political advocacy toward Storting/UD/Norad, and corporate partnerships.
- **Funds emergency appeals** (Ukraine 2022 drove the NOK 464m income spike; Sudan, Gaza, Syria-earthquake follow the same pattern).

UNICEF itself — the intergovernmental fund UNICEF-komiteen represents — operates in **190+ countries and territories** with ~13 000 employees (90% field-based). Norwegian private donations routed through the committee land in that global network; the committee does not publish its own country-level expenditure split because it doesn't operate country programmes — UNICEF International does.

### Mission 2 — Domestic: children's-rights programme in Norway

UNICEF-komiteen also runs a domestic children's-rights programme. This is where it diverges from the pure fundraising-affiliate model used by, say, SOS-barnebyer Norge. Programme areas (verified on unicef.no/vart-arbeid/norge):

| Programme | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Kommuneanalysen** | Annual analysis of whether Norway's 357 kommuner prioritise children and young people across five sectors (barnehage, grunnskole, barnevern, helsetjenester, kulturtilbud). 2025 edition used 2024 data and ranked Salangen as Norway's best oppvekstkommune. Often generates national media coverage. |
| **Barnevennlige kommuner** | Aligned with UNICEF International's global Child Friendly Cities Initiative (CFCI). Norwegian implementation has been lighter than the global framework — more a principle UNICEF Norge promotes than a formal certification with an active Norwegian roster. |
| **Familiefattigdom** | Child-poverty advocacy and policy work, with increasingly prominent public-facing presence under Greenberg Bergheim's tenure. |
| **Digital oppvekst** | Children's rights in digital environments; screen time, social media, online safety. |
| **Rasisme** | Rights-focused work on minority-background children and youth in Norway. |
| **LHBT+ rettigheter for barn** | Children's rights intersection with sexual-orientation/gender-identity protections. |
| **UNICEF Norges ungdomspanel** | 15–20-year-old panel that advises UNICEF Norge and meets decision-makers. Current (2026) panel: Herman 15, Ronit 17, Ingrid 17, Hadia 18, Dino 18, Selma 20. Led by Kristin Wee Sævig. |
| **Barnas tale** | Annual children's address presented before the King's and Prime Minister's New Year speeches — high-visibility Norwegian civic ritual. |

**Not a UNICEF Norge programme**: *Barnas klimapanel* is run by **Miljøagentene** (Framtiden i våre hender's youth arm), not UNICEF. Occasionally confused in secondary sources. UNICEF Norge's own climate-and-children work goes through Kommuneanalysen sector-five indicators and through the Ungdomspanel, not through Barnas klimapanel.

### Flagship initiatives

- **Kommuneanalysen** — the domestic flagship. Annual publication; annual media cycle. A credible, media-amplified NGO voice on kommune-level equality for children.
- **UNICEF-runden** — school and kindergarten activity-day fundraiser. Pupils collect pledges for completing a physical activity; the money funds UNICEF global programmes. Run nationally each spring via Spleis.
- **Barnas tale** — the children's speech before the King's and PM's New Year's addresses; one of UNICEF Norge's highest-profile public-advocacy formats.
- **Faddere (sponsor-a-child)** — the revenue backbone. UNICEF Norge's fadder programme is the dominant private-income stream (a majority of the NOK 202m "innsamlede midler" in 2024). No specific fadder count is published on the site in 2026-04, but scale is broadly comparable to Plan International Norge (115 000) and SOS-barnebyer Norge (~100 000 givers).

## Where they work

UNICEF-komiteen Norge itself works **from one office in Oslo**. The field operations funded by Norwegian donations run under **UNICEF International**, which is active in **190+ countries and territories** and maintains a presence in virtually every jurisdiction where UN-coordinated children's programming is relevant.

UNICEF-komiteen does not publish its own country-level expenditure split on unicef.no. For country-level impact of Norwegian private money, the best public data is:

- **UNICEF International annual report** (`unicef.org/reports`) — global + country programme spend.
- **UNICEF International Humanitarian Action for Children (HAC)** appeals — earmarked emergency allocations.
- **Norway-to-UNICEF commitment statements** — Norway (the state) also commits separately to UNICEF via UD/Norad framework agreements (e.g. the USD 150m / 3-year core-funding commitment announced 2023). That flow is *not* routed through UNICEF-komiteen Norge; it's a direct MFA-to-UNICEF transfer and is not part of the 250m income discussed in this profile.

This distinction matters for the transparency story: **Norway (state) → UNICEF International** and **Norwegian private donors → UNICEF-komiteen Norge → UNICEF International** are two separate money pipes, and only the second is what UNICEF-komiteen's 250m income represents.

## Money in — funding composition

This is the most distinctive data point for UNICEF-komiteen: it is one of the very few large Norwegian NGOs that **does not receive Norwegian state operational grants by organisational policy**. The only other organisation in the same bracket is Leger Uten Grenser Norge (private-only since 2016). The statement is explicit on unicef.no/om/hva-gar-pengene-til: "All our collected funds come from private individuals and businesses."

### What each line actually represents

- **Innsamlede midler (NOK 202.3m, 80.8% of income)** — fadder-programmet, fast givere, enkeltbeløp, testament, korporativt, UNICEF-runden. This is the policy-relevant "private" number.
- **Offentlige tilskudd (NOK 6.9m, 2.8%)** — primarily **momskompensasjon** (VAT-refund ordning that every registered Norwegian NGO is eligible for regardless of funding policy). Not "state funding" in the operational-grant sense the `no Norwegian state money` policy refers to.
- **Andre tilskudd (NOK 23.6m, 9.4%)** — institutional grants from UNICEF International to cover specific Norway-committee projects, plus Stiftelsen Dam and similar private foundations. The UNICEF HQ share here is the committee receiving earmarked programme support from its own international parent, not Norwegian public money.
- **Andre inntekter (NOK 17.6m, 7.0%)** — finance income, FX gains, other non-programmatic.

### The "no state funding" claim — verified and nuanced

UNICEF Norge's public position (hva-gar-pengene-til): **"Vi er en uavhengig organisasjon i FN, og får ingen midler fra FN. Alle våre innsamlede midler kommer fra privatpersoner og bedrifter."** The "hva går pengene til" page also states that the formålsprosent is not directly comparable to other Norwegian NGOs because UNICEF Norge takes no state operational support.

This is consistent with the absence of Norad or UD framework agreements on UNICEF Norge's books (unlike Kirkens Nødhjelp's ~350m/yr Norad, Norsk Folkehjelp's Norad frames, or Plan/Redd Barna's Norad rammeavtaler). Momskompensasjon (6.9m) is a tax-refund, not a discretionary state grant, and no other Norwegian state ordning shows on the activity account.

**Caveat**: UNICEF Norge's FAQ page (ofte-stilte-sporsmal) has a separate, slightly contradictory phrasing that says the organisation depends on "statlige overføringer og donasjoner". Reading both pages together, the consistent operational truth is: **no Norwegian state operational funding** to UNICEF Norge the committee; Norway the state separately funds UNICEF International directly via UD/Norad, which is the "statlige overføringer" the FAQ is referring to in the broader UNICEF-system context. Editorial note for app surfaces: use the "no Norwegian state money to the committee" framing and footnote the Norway→UNICEF-International flow separately.

### Top revenue channels (narrative)

- **Fadderprogrammet** — sponsor-a-child. The dominant private-income channel; monthly AvtaleGiro recurring giving.
- **Fast giver / enkeltbeløp** — non-sponsorship recurring and one-off donations.
- **Korporative partnerskap** — business partnerships (partnership income not separately itemised on Innsamlingskontrollen).
- **Verdensgaver** — symbolic-gift webshop (mosquito nets, RUTF sachets, school kits).
- **Testamentariske gaver (arv)** — bequest income.
- **UNICEF-runden** — annual schools fundraiser via Spleis.

### Funding flows outside Lottstift

Because UNICEF-komiteen receives no Norwegian state operational grants, a naive Lottstift query for 915972438 returns essentially only momskompensasjon. Unlike NRC or Kirkens Nødhjelp (where the state money lives outside Lottstift in UD/Norad framework agreements), for UNICEF-komiteen **there is no hidden state funding outside Lottstift either** — the "no state money" policy is genuine. This makes UNICEF Norge and Leger Uten Grenser Norge the two cleanest "purely private" case studies in the Norwegian humanitarian-NGO set.

Sum of costs exceeds income in 2024 — the deficit is covered from accumulated reserves built in the Ukraine-spike year 2022.

UNICEF-komiteen does not publish a Norwegian-side programme split (education vs. WASH vs. vaccines vs. emergencies), because its direct-purpose costs are primarily *transfers to UNICEF International* plus a minority for the Norwegian domestic programme. Programme-thematic impact reporting lives in UNICEF International's annual report, not the Norwegian committee's accounts.

Fundraising cost share is high relative to peers (NRC ~1.5%, Folkehjelp ~5%, Kirkens Nødhjelp ~8%) — this is the known trade-off of a **pure private-donor model with no state operational base**. Share-to-cause (71%) is correspondingly lower than operationally-funded peers. The `ngo-landscape.md` baseline of 66.3% looks to be from an older measurement cycle (possibly 2022 when Ukraine-spike inflated ratios); 2024 calc from published line items is 71.1%. Both numbers are defensible depending on which methodology; pick one and label.

## How to engage

UNICEF-komiteen is **donor-first** for the Norwegian public. There is no member, no lokallag, no volunteer pathway, no school-ambassador-lite programme beyond the school-fundraising materials. The Ungdomspanel is a **programmatic participation** path (young people advise UNICEF Norge on policy), not a volunteering opportunity in the operational sense.

Verified pathways (unicef.no/om/ofte-stilte-sporsmal and /stott/privat):

| Pathway | Route | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fadder (child sponsor) | `unicef.no/stott/privat/fadder` | Primary recurring-giver format; AvtaleGiro |
| Fast giver (non-sponsor) | `unicef.no/stott/privat/fast-giver` | Recurring non-sponsored |
| Enkeltbeløp | `unicef.no/stott/privat` | Vipps, card, bank account 1644 04 09400 |
| Testamentarisk gave (arv) | unicef.no testament link | Bequest |
| Minnegave | unicef.no minnegave link | Memorial gift |
| Verdensgaver (symbolic gifts) | unicef.no/verdensgaver | Mosquito nets, vaccines, education kits |
| Korporativ partner | `unicef.no/stott/bedrift` | Corporate partnerships |
| UNICEF-runden (school/kindergarten) | spleis.no/unicefrunden | National school fundraising |
| Tax deduction | 500–25 000 NOK qualifies for skattefradrag | Norwegian rules |

Explicitly **not** available for the Norwegian public:
- No membership (stiftelse — no members by form).
- No lokallag / domestic chapter network.
- **No volunteer placement**: "Vi setter pris på at mange ønsker å engasjere seg i UNICEFs arbeid, men vi tar dessverre ikke imot frivillige i Norge" (unicef.no/om/ofte-stilte-sporsmal). International volunteering is pointed toward UN Volunteers (UNV).
- No signature/petition pathway at the organisational level (issue campaigns run through advocacy channels and op-eds rather than public petitions).

### Why no volunteer pathway — three structural reasons

1. **Operational model.** UNICEF's field operations are run by UNICEF International with paid international and national staff. The Norwegian committee's role is fundraising + advocacy + domestic rights programme; there is no operational vehicle for volunteer contribution.
2. **Stiftelse form.** Foundations have no members; the only governance bodies are the styre and the SG. Volunteer involvement would require a separate organisational structure (as Norsk Folkehjelp and Røde Kors maintain with their lokallag).
3. **Domestic programme profile.** Kommuneanalysen, Ungdomspanel, and Barnas tale are research/policy/youth-participation work, not activity-based operations. Norwegian volunteers who want child-focused work are systematically pointed toward **Redd Barna's aktivitetsgrupper**, **Røde Kors Ung and leksehjelp/flyktningsguide**, or local **BUA / barnehage / SFO** volunteering.

For the app, this means UNICEF-komiteen's card in the Compare-NGOs view should explicitly say "donasjonsbasert stiftelse — ingen lokallag, ingen frivilligplassering, ingen medlemskap" rather than show empty volunteer / member slots.

## Caveats and gaps

- **Share-to-cause discrepancy.** `ngo-landscape.md` cites 66.3%; Innsamlingskontrollen's own 5-year innsamlingsprosent displays as 61.3% on the org page; unicef.no/om/hva-gar-pengene-til cites 76.8% (5-year avg) and 4% admin. The 2024 line-item calculation from the activity account is 71.1% (177 976 / 250 376). All four are different views of overlapping metrics — the Ukraine-spike year 2022 distorts 5-year averages. Pick one and label methodology; recommend **71.1% (2024 activity-account calc)** for annual comparability.
- **UNICEF Norge FAQ contradicts the "hva går pengene til" page** on state-money phrasing. Resolution: the FAQ's "statlige overføringer" refers to Norway→UNICEF-International at the UN-system level, not Norway→UNICEF-komiteen. The committee itself takes no Norwegian state operational money. Editorial consistency check advised.
- **No fadder count published.** Unlike Plan (115 000) and SOS (~100 000), UNICEF Norge does not publish a current fadder count on unicef.no. The NOK 202m innsamlede midler line gives an indirect size estimate (consistent with ~100 000+ faddere at typical monthly giving levels).
- **Barnevennlige kommuner programme is lighter than it looks.** Live search for currently-certified Norwegian municipalities returns few results; most activity is on Kommuneanalysen, not formal CFCI certification. The task-brief framing of "Barnevennlig kommune programme (certification for Norwegian municipalities)" overstates how active the certification element is in 2026. Kommuneanalysen is the real flagship.
- **Employee count variance.** Brreg antallAnsatte is 64 (authoritative for this profile); Wikipedia no.wiki cites "ca. 45–50" which is stale.
- **Income volatility.** 2022 income was NOK 464m, driven by Ukraine; 2024 is NOK 250m. Any longitudinal display must show the 2022 outlier with context.
- **Annual accounts PDF parsing failed** for 2024 (returned 404; the 2023 PDF is live). Programme/geographical spend split exists only inside UNICEF International's annual report, not the Norwegian committee's accounts.
- **Founding-year ambiguity.** Brreg says 2015-07-02 (current legal entity, stiftelse re-registration). Organisational history goes back to **1954** as the Norwegian national committee. App should display "Founded 1954" with a footnote about the 2015 stiftelse re-registration.
- **"Public grants 6.9m" interpretation.** This is predominantly momskompensasjon (a tax-refund ordning) rather than operational state grants; the distinction matters for the "no state money" story.
- **"Andre tilskudd 23.6m" composition.** Not itemised on the Innsamlingskontrollen summary; annual-report PDF likely breaks it down between UNICEF HQ transfers, Dam, and other private-foundation grants. Worth a follow-up PDF parse.
- **Corporate partner list not extracted** in this verification pass. unicef.no/partner lists active corporates; would be relevant for a future revision.
